# Yoga

## THERAPY: yoga

### Evidence Grade
**[Moderate]** for quality of life, stress, disease activity in UC; less CD-specific data but benefit plausible and safe.

### Key Trials

**Cramer H, et al. 2017** — *Aliment Pharmacol Ther* 45(11):1379-89. Randomized trial: yoga (n=39) vs written self-care (n=38) in UC in clinical remission. 12 weekly 90-min **Hatha yoga** sessions. 
- Disease activity significantly lower in yoga group at 12 and 24 weeks
- Quality of life (IBDQ) improved more in yoga
- Perceived stress mediator confirmed in secondary analysis (Evers et al. 2019)
- Largest UC yoga RCT. (PMID for Cramer 2017: 28378342 — verify when possible.)

**Sharma P, et al. 2015** (PMID: 26667293): Yoga intervention in IBD — positive quality-of-life signals.

**Pediatric IBD yoga trial** (Arruda et al. 2024, PMID: 38327225): prospective controlled multisite — improvements in anxiety, stress, quality of life.

### Mechanism
- Parasympathetic activation (breath, posture)
- HPA axis modulation (cortisol reduction)
- Vagal tone improvement (HRV studies)
- Stress/anxiety reduction (documented mediator of IBD symptoms)
- Physical activity benefits (modest)

### Dose
- 60-90 minute sessions 2-3x/week, minimum 8-12 weeks
- **Gentle/restorative/Hatha styles preferred** — avoid hot yoga, intense vinyasa during flares (dehydration, physical stress)
- Avoid deep abdominal twists in active flares / post-op

### Safety
- Generally very safe with competent instructor
- Musculoskeletal strain (modify for joint issues, osteoporosis from steroid use)
- Avoid inversions if uncontrolled HTN, retinal issues

### Practical Bottom Line
**One of the highest-yield "complementary" interventions** — stress is a documented flare trigger, quality of life matters, side effects minimal. Recommend gentle/Hatha style, 2-3x/week.
