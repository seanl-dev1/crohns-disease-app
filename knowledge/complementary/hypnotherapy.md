# Gut-Directed Hypnotherapy (GDH)

## THERAPY: gut-directed-hypnotherapy

### Evidence Grade
**[Strong for IBS]** — Peter Whorwell's Manchester model is well-validated. **[Moderate]** for UC (maintenance). **[Weak-Emerging]** for CD.

### Background
**Peter Whorwell** (South Manchester, UK) pioneered scripted hypnosis for IBS in the early 1980s. The "Manchester protocol" is the most validated GDH script. 7-12 sessions over 3 months with home recordings. Focus: gut relaxation imagery, reduced visceral hypersensitivity, restoring normal motility perception.

### Evidence
- **Whorwell PJ, et al. 1984**: original IBS trial — profound benefits, replicated many times. IBS remains the cleanest indication.
- **Mawdsley JE, et al. 2008** (UC, PMID: 18637160): reduced mucosal inflammation acutely.
- **Keefer L, et al. 2013** — Journal of Clinical Gastroenterology: randomized trial of GDH in UC in remission. **7 sessions over 3 months**. Reduced frequency of clinical flares vs control. **Laurie Keefer** at Mount Sinai is the US IBD-specific GDH pioneer.
- **CD trial — Beesley A, et al. 2023** (PMID: 37531719): virtual adjunctive GDH pilot/feasibility RCT in CD. Feasible, well-received (79% positive experience), quality-of-life signals. Not powered for efficacy.

### Dose
- 7-12 sessions over 3 months with licensed therapist trained in GDH
- OR apps: **Nerva** (IBS-validated), **Mahana IBS** (FDA-cleared) — adapted protocols, lower cost
- Home practice 15-20 min daily

### Safety
- Very safe; rare dissociative/emotional emergence — therapist handles
- Not effective for everyone (~30-40% non-responders in IBS trials)

### Practical Bottom Line
Underutilized. Excellent evidence for **coexisting IBS** (common in CD patients with healed mucosa but ongoing symptoms — ~40% of quiescent IBD has IBS overlap). **Sean's presentation** (post-resection, 20 years) may have functional overlay from altered anatomy/dysmotility — GDH could be particularly valuable for him. Find GDH-trained therapist via International Society of Hypnosis or the ACG/Rome Foundation directories. Apps are a reasonable starting point.
