# Slippery Elm / DGL (Licorice)

## SUPPLEMENT: slippery-elm-dgl

### Evidence Grade
**[Anecdotal-to-Weak]** — traditional use, mechanistic rationale, no RCT evidence specific to CD.

### Slippery Elm (*Ulmus rubra*)
- Inner bark powder, high mucilage content
- **Mechanism**: forms viscous gel on mucosal surfaces, mechanical protection, possible mild anti-inflammatory. Not systemically absorbed.
- **Evidence**: one small uncontrolled trial in UC (Langmead et al. 2002) of herbal combinations including slippery elm showed some benefit. No RCT. Traditional Native American use for GI/respiratory ailments well documented.
- **Dose**: 400-500 mg capsules 2-3x/day, OR 1-2 teaspoons powder mixed in water as slurry.
- **Safety**: generally safe; may delay absorption of concomitant oral meds (take 1-2 hours apart). Endangered tree species in some regions — ethical sourcing concern.

### Deglycyrrhizinated Licorice (DGL)
- *Glycyrrhiza glabra* root with glycyrrhizin removed (to avoid mineralocorticoid effects — hypertension, hypokalemia, edema).
- **Mechanism**: increases mucin production, may inhibit H. pylori, antioxidant. Effects primarily upper GI (stomach/duodenum) rather than colon.
- **Evidence**: older trials in peptic ulcer disease (pre-PPI era) showed modest benefit. No IBD-specific RCT.
- **Dose**: 380-760 mg chewed 20 min before meals (buccal absorption).
- **Safety (DGL)**: generally safe. **WHOLE licorice or regular licorice root** (glycyrrhizin-containing): pseudoaldosteronism, HTN, hypokalemia — avoid if >100 mg glycyrrhizin/day or chronic use.

### Drug Interactions
- **Whole licorice**: diuretics (hypokalemia), digoxin, antihypertensives, corticosteroids (potentiation), warfarin
- **DGL**: minimal

### Practical Bottom Line
Both are low-risk mucosal soothers with weak evidence. Slippery elm more relevant for lower GI / IBD context. DGL more for dyspepsia/gastritis. Unlikely to be disease-modifying but reasonable for symptom palliation. **Use DGL specifically — never whole licorice extract chronically.**
