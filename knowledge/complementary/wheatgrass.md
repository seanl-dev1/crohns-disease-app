# Wheatgrass Juice

## SUPPLEMENT: wheatgrass

### Evidence Grade
**[Weak]** — one small positive RCT in **UC only**, no CD data.

### The Single RCT
**Ben-Arye E, et al. 2002** — *Scand J Gastroenterol* 37(4):444-9. **PMID: 11989836**. Israeli double-blind placebo-controlled RCT in **active distal UC** (not CD). n=23 (small). **100 mL fresh wheatgrass juice/day × 1 month** vs placebo.
- Disease Activity Index decreased significantly (p=0.03)
- Rectal bleeding decreased (p=0.03)
- Physician global assessment improved (p=0.03)
- Side effects: nausea common; otherwise well tolerated

### Mechanism (speculated)
- Chlorophyll, antioxidants (polyphenols, flavonoids), superoxide dismutase (though most is destroyed by stomach acid)
- No convincing mechanistic data specific to IBD

### Dose
If used: 100 mL fresh juice daily (as in Ben-Arye trial). Fresh > powdered.

### Safety
- Generally safe; nausea common
- Mold/bacterial contamination risk in improperly grown wheatgrass — reliable source important
- Wheat allergy — despite being "before grain formation," cross-reactivity possible; caution with celiac

### Practical Bottom Line
One small positive trial in UC; no data in CD. Low risk. **Don't oversell.** Reasonable adjunct if patient enjoys and tolerates it — unlikely to hurt, modest plausibility of benefit.
