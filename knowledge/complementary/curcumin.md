# Curcumin / Turmeric

## SUPPLEMENT: curcumin

### Evidence Grade
- **[Moderate]** for ulcerative colitis (induction + maintenance adjunct to mesalamine)
- **[Moderate]** for Crohn's disease (one positive RCT with high-bioavailability form; one negative postoperative RCT)

### Mechanism
Curcumin is the principal polyphenol (curcuminoid) in *Curcuma longa* (turmeric). Mechanistic actions relevant to IBD:
- **NF-κB inhibition** — blocks IκB kinase, preventing NF-κB nuclear translocation; downregulates TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, COX-2, iNOS transcription.
- **STAT3 inhibition** — reduces Th17 differentiation.
- **Nrf2 activation** — upregulates antioxidant response elements (HO-1, NQO1).
- **5-LOX / COX-2 inhibition** — reduces leukotriene/prostaglandin synthesis.
- **TLR4 signaling modulation.**
- **Microbiome effects** — small human studies suggest modest alteration of Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratios.

### Key RCTs (Real PMIDs verified)

**Ulcerative Colitis — MAINTENANCE**
- **Hanai H, et al. 2006** — *Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol* 4(12):1502-6. **PMID: 17101300**. Double-blind, multicenter, placebo-controlled RCT, 89 patients with quiescent UC on mesalamine/sulfasalazine. Curcumin **2 g/day × 6 months**. Relapse: **4.65% curcumin vs 20.51% placebo** (p=0.04). Significant reduction in clinical activity index and endoscopic index.

**Ulcerative Colitis — INDUCTION (adjunct)**
- **Lang A, et al. 2015** — *Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol* 13(8):1444-9. **PMID: 25724700**. Multicenter RCT, 50 patients with mild-to-moderate active UC on mesalamine. Curcumin **3 g/day × 1 month**. Clinical remission: **53.8% curcumin + mesalamine vs 0% placebo + mesalamine** (p=0.01). Endoscopic remission: 38% vs 0%.
- **Kedia S, et al. 2017** — Indian RCT of curcumin 450 mg/day — **negative** (no benefit over placebo). Likely dose too low given bioavailability issues.

**Crohn's Disease — INDUCTION**
- **Sugimoto K, et al. 2020** — *J Crohns Colitis* 14(12):1693-1701. **PMID: 32412598**. Randomized, double-blind, multicenter (5 Japanese sites). **Theracurmin® 360 mg/day × 12 weeks** (highly bioavailable nanoparticle form, ~27x absorption of standard curcumin). n=30 (20 active, 10 placebo) with mild-to-moderate CD. Clinical remission at week 12: **40% vs 0%** (p=0.02). Endoscopic remission: 15% vs 0%. Significant healing of anal lesions at week 8. No serious adverse events. **This is the cleanest CD-specific curcumin RCT to date.**

**Crohn's Disease — POSTOPERATIVE PROPHYLAXIS (NEGATIVE)**
- **Bommelaer G, et al. 2020** — *Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol* 18(7):1553-1560. **PMID: 31470175**. French multicenter double-blind RCT, 62 post-resection CD patients on azathioprine. Oral curcumin **3 g/day × 6 months**. Postoperative recurrence (Rutgeerts ≥i2): **58% curcumin vs 68% placebo** (p=0.60, NS). Concerning signal: **severe recurrence (Rutgeerts ≥i3) 55% curcumin vs 26% placebo** (p=0.034) — possibly chance with small n, but worth noting. SAEs 16% vs 6%. Honest conclusion: curcumin did NOT prevent postoperative recurrence.

### Bioavailability — The Critical Issue
Unformulated curcumin has ~1% oral bioavailability due to poor aqueous solubility, rapid glucuronidation/sulfation, and biliary excretion. Almost all serum concentrations achievable with plain turmeric powder are sub-therapeutic for anti-inflammatory effect.

**Enhancement strategies:**
- **Piperine** (black pepper alkaloid) — Shoba et al. 1998 (PMID: 9619120) showed piperine 20 mg boosts curcumin bioavailability ~2000% (20-fold) in humans via glucuronidation inhibition. Most classic "curcumin + piperine" products use this. However, 2025 pharmacokinetic reappraisal suggests benefit may be overstated when compared to modern formulations. Also — piperine itself inhibits CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein, potentially affecting other drug levels.
- **Meriva®** (curcumin-phosphatidylcholine phytosome) — ~29x bioavailability vs standard curcumin. Best evidence in osteoarthritis, some IBD signal.
- **Theracurmin®** — colloidal nanoparticle dispersion, ~27x bioavailability. Used in Sugimoto 2020 CD trial.
- **BCM-95/Curcugreen** — turmeric essential oil + curcumin, ~7-10x bioavailability.
- **Longvida®** — solid lipid curcumin particle, ~65x bioavailability for free curcumin.
- **Nanocurcumin** (liposomal) — variable evidence.

### Effective Dose
- **Standard curcumin**: 2-3 g/day (with piperine 5-20 mg) — this is the Hanai/Lang regimen
- **Theracurmin**: 360 mg/day (much lower mg due to higher absorption) — Sugimoto regimen
- **Meriva**: 500-1000 mg twice daily

### Safety & Risks
**Hepatotoxicity (emerging concern):**
- **Likoudis P, et al. 2022** — *Am J Med* 135(11):1340-1347. **PMID: 36252717**. DILIN case series: **10 cases of turmeric-associated liver injury**, 8 women, median age 56. 9 hepatocellular pattern, 1 mixed. **5 hospitalized, 1 death from acute liver failure.** 7 of 10 carried **HLA-B*35:01** allele (same allele implicated in green tea, *Polygonum multiflorum*, and *Garcinia cambogia* hepatotoxicity). 3 of 7 tested products contained piperine — suggesting piperine-enhanced absorption may increase hepatotoxic risk.
- NIH LiverTox now lists turmeric as the **most common cause of herbal-related liver injury in the US** (as of 2022).
- Latency typically weeks to months. Usually resolves with discontinuation but can be severe.

**Bleeding risk:**
- Curcumin has antiplatelet and mild anticoagulant effects (decreases thromboxane A2, may inhibit platelet aggregation).
- Multiple case reports of INR elevation in patients on warfarin taking turmeric — one report of INR >10.
- Avoid or closely monitor in patients on warfarin, DOACs, heparin, antiplatelets (aspirin, clopidogrel), or perioperatively (stop 2 weeks before surgery).

**GI effects:**
- Doses >4 g/day may cause nausea, diarrhea, reflux.
- Can increase bile flow — caution with gallstones, bile duct obstruction.

**Other:**
- Oxalate content — may increase kidney stone risk in predisposed individuals.
- Iron chelation — may worsen iron-deficiency anemia with high-dose chronic use.
- Theoretical: CYP3A4, CYP2D6, P-gp modulation — may affect many drugs.

### Drug Interactions (Curcumin)
- **Warfarin** — increased INR, bleeding
- **Antiplatelets** — additive bleeding
- **Tacrolimus, cyclosporine** — possible level changes via CYP3A4
- **Chemotherapy** — varies (paradoxically may enhance some; antagonize others)
- **Iron supplements** — reduced absorption

### Practical Bottom Line
**For UC**: Reasonable adjunct to mesalamine — decent evidence for maintenance (Hanai) and induction (Lang).
**For CD**: Theracurmin at 360 mg/day has the best CD-specific data. Standard curcumin 2-3 g/day is reasonable but less validated. **Do not use in lieu of induction therapy for moderate-severe CD.** Monitor LFTs every 3-6 months with chronic use. Avoid with anticoagulants without physician oversight. **Stop immediately if fatigue, jaundice, dark urine, or RUQ pain.**
