# Omega-3 Fatty Acids (EPA/DHA)

## SUPPLEMENT: omega-3

### Evidence Grade
**[Weak]** for CD disease activity / remission maintenance. Two large well-powered RCTs failed to show benefit. Some mechanistic rationale persists; general cardiovascular/mental health benefits remain.

### Mechanism
- **EPA (eicosapentaenoic acid)** — precursor to series-3 prostaglandins and series-5 leukotrienes (less inflammatory than arachidonic acid-derived series-2 prostaglandins and series-4 leukotrienes).
- **DHA (docosahexaenoic acid)** — structural membrane lipid, precursor to resolvins and protectins (specialized pro-resolving mediators — SPMs).
- **PPAR-γ activation, NF-κB inhibition, reduced endotoxin-induced TNF-α.**
- **Substrate competition** with arachidonic acid at COX/LOX enzymes.

### Landmark Trials — The EPIC Disappointment

**EPIC-1 and EPIC-2 — Feagan BG, et al. 2008** — *JAMA* 299(14):1690-7. **PMID: 18398081**.
- Two parallel multicenter (98 sites, Canada/Europe/Israel/US), double-blind, placebo-controlled RCTs. Total n=738 (EPIC-1: 363, EPIC-2: 375) CD patients in remission.
- Enteric-coated **omega-3 free fatty acids 4 g/day** (2.2 g EPA + 0.8 g DHA) × up to 58 weeks.
- EPIC-1 relapse at 1 year: **31.6% omega-3 vs 35.7% placebo** (p=0.30).
- EPIC-2 relapse at 1 year: **47.8% omega-3 vs 48.8% placebo** (p=0.48).
- **Omega-3 did NOT maintain remission in CD.** Definitive, well-powered, negative.

**Cochrane Review — Lev-Tzion R, et al. 2014** — *Cochrane Database Syst Rev.* **PMID: 24585498** / also **PMID: 8988157**. Systematic review of 6 RCTs (n=1,039). Conclusion: Omega-3 fatty acids are **probably ineffective** for maintenance of remission in CD. GI adverse events (diarrhea, dyspepsia) and fish-burp more common.

Earlier contradictory signal: Belluzzi A, et al. 1996 (*NEJM*) — smaller enteric-coated fish oil trial showed ~40% reduction in relapse — but not replicated in EPIC trials with larger n and better methodology.

### Dose
If used (for cardiometabolic/general anti-inflammatory reasons, not CD-specific):
- **1-4 g/day combined EPA+DHA**
- Fish oil at 4 g/day is approved (Lovaza/Vascepa) for hypertriglyceridemia
- Food equivalent: **2-3 servings fatty fish/week** (salmon, sardines, mackerel, herring, anchovies)
- Vegan sources: algae-derived DHA/EPA (~300-600 mg/day); ALA from flax/chia/walnuts has poor conversion (~0.5-5% to EPA, <0.5% to DHA)

### Safety
- **Bleeding risk** — dose-dependent antiplatelet effect; clinically minor at typical doses but avoid high dose with anticoagulants or perioperatively.
- **Fishy aftertaste, eructation** — mitigated with enteric coating, freezing capsules, taking with meals.
- **Atrial fibrillation** — recent signal at high doses (>4 g/day, as in REDUCE-IT) — unclear if relevant for CD context.
- **Heavy metal/PCB contamination** — use third-party tested brands (IFOS certified).
- **Oxidation/rancidity** — check peroxide/TOTOX values; don't use if fishy-smelling capsule.

### Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin / DOACs / antiplatelets** — additive bleeding risk at >3 g/day
- **Antihypertensives** — mild additive BP lowering

### Practical Bottom Line
**Do NOT rely on omega-3 to maintain CD remission** — EPIC disproved this. Reasonable as part of dietary pattern (Mediterranean-style eating) for general cardiometabolic health. If supplementing, 1-2 g/day EPA+DHA is reasonable and safe. Eating oily fish 2-3x/week is probably the best approach. **This is not a disease-modifying intervention for CD.**
