# Interactions with Standard Crohn's Meds

## SAFETY: drug-interactions

### Azathioprine / 6-Mercaptopurine (6-MP)
- Metabolized via TPMT → 6-thioguanine nucleotides (active); xanthine oxidase → inactive metabolites
- **Allopurinol (or febuxostat)** — inhibits xanthine oxidase → massive increase in active metabolites → **life-threatening bone marrow suppression**. If combined (intentionally for "low-dose AZA + allopurinol" strategy to correct skewed metabolism), AZA reduced to 25-33% of dose, strict CBC monitoring.
- **Supplements that may theoretically interact**:
  - High-dose vitamin C (gram doses): uric acid effects theoretical — probably minor
  - Sulfasalazine/mesalamine: weak TPMT inhibition — standard in IBD combos, not a problem
  - Warfarin: AZA can affect warfarin levels
- **Natural health products**: generally no major interactions reported, but AZA has narrow therapeutic index — **avoid starting new supplements without discussing with GI**
- **Liver and marrow monitoring** essential (CBC, LFT every 3 months)

### Methotrexate (MTX)
- Folate antagonist (inhibits dihydrofolate reductase)
- **Folic acid 1 mg/day** or 5 mg/week (non-MTX days) — reduces side effects without substantially reducing efficacy; standard practice
- **Do NOT take folic acid same day as MTX** — theoretical efficacy reduction
- **NSAIDs** can increase MTX toxicity (reduce clearance) — avoid
- **Alcohol** increases hepatotoxicity — limit/avoid
- Penicillins, sulfonamides, high-dose aspirin — increase MTX toxicity
- **Supplements that interact**:
  - Nothing major and consistent
  - Liver-supporting herbs (milk thistle) — mostly safe, may theoretically protect LFTs but shouldn't enable higher MTX doses

### Biologics (anti-TNF: Infliximab, Adalimumab, Certolizumab, Golimumab; anti-integrin: Vedolizumab; anti-IL12/23: Ustekinumab; anti-IL23: Risankizumab, Mirikizumab; JAK inhibitors: Tofacitinib, Upadacitinib)
- **Biologic PK is through protein turnover, not CYP-based** — most supplement interactions are minimal
- **JAK inhibitors** ARE CYP3A4 substrates — CBD, grapefruit juice, St. John's wort, strong CYP inhibitors/inducers matter
- **Immunosuppression combined with natural product immunostimulants** (Echinacea, astragalus, ashwagandha) — theoretical reduction in efficacy; evidence weak
- **Live probiotics + immunosuppression** — small infection risk (fungemia for S. boulardii); discuss before starting
- **Vaccines + natural immune stimulants** — no strong data, likely minor

### Corticosteroids (Prednisone, Budesonide)
- Licorice (glycyrrhizin-containing) potentiates — hypokalemia, HTN, Cushingoid
- Vitamin D, calcium — essential supplementation (osteoporosis prevention)
- Melatonin — counteracts some cortisol effects; generally safe
- Avoid NSAIDs (additive GI/renal risk)

### Mesalamine / Sulfasalazine
- Well tolerated; minor interactions
- Can reduce folate absorption (sulfasalazine specifically) — supplement folate
- Monitor renal function (rare interstitial nephritis)
- Curcumin + mesalamine: studied combinations — positive synergy in trials

### General Principles for Supplement-Drug Interactions
1. **Report all supplements to GI, pharmacist, PCP** — the medication list on the chart must include them
2. **Check for CYP-mediated interactions** — especially CYP3A4, CYP2C9 (turmeric, CBD, St. John's wort, grapefruit, goldenseal)
3. **Stop supplements 1-2 weeks before surgery** — particularly those with bleeding potential (curcumin, fish oil, ginkgo, garlic, ginseng, vitamin E, ginger)
4. **Be cautious about layering immunomodulating interventions** — biologics + high-dose curcumin + CBD + etc. = unknown cumulative effects
5. **Monitor labs**: CBC, LFTs, creatinine, vitamin levels, fecal calprotectin — objective data is everything
