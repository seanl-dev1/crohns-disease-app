# Form-Matched Food Substitution Library

**Purpose:** When the scanner flags a food RED or YELLOW, replacement suggestions must pass the "could you mistake this for the same food type on a plate" test. Form factor, eating occasion, and craving category all matter. "Better ingredient choice" alone is not enough.

**Tier definitions**
- **Tier 1** — Near-identical form, Crohn's-friendlier ingredients. Primary suggestions. Someone scanning Skittles wants chewy sweet candy; someone scanning pizza wants pizza.
- **Tier 2** — Close cousins. Same eating occasion and craving, different specific form. OK as secondary suggestions.
- **Tier 3** — Reframe. Genuinely different food that addresses an adjacent craving. Must be explicitly labeled as "if you're ready to shift away from X." Never the primary suggestion.

**What makes a food Crohn's-friendlier**
- Low/no lactose (or aged cheese where lactose is minimal)
- Low insoluble fiber (cooked, peeled, refined where helpful)
- No sugar alcohols (sorbitol, xylitol, mannitol, maltitol, erythritol)
- No emulsifiers with strong IBD signals: carrageenan, polysorbate 80, carboxymethylcellulose (CMC)
- No high-fructose corn syrup (osmotic diarrhea risk; mild microbiome concern)
- Lower total fat (post-resection bile acid tolerance is limited)
- No trans fats; minimal hydrogenated oils
- Low spice/capsaicin for active-flare users
- No alcohol during flares; moderation otherwise
- Omega-3 rich where substituting fat sources (anti-inflammatory)

**When there is no good Tier 1 swap**
Flag the category as "limit or avoid during flares, small portions in remission" rather than invent a bad swap.

## Breakfast

### Sugary cereal (Froot Loops, Cap'n Crunch, Lucky Charms, Frosted Flakes, Cocoa Puffs)
Common triggers: HFCS, high sugar, colorings, sometimes emulsifiers, low fiber is actually helpful here but the sugar load causes osmotic diarrhea.

**Tier 1**
- Rice Chex (plain) — minimal ingredients, no HFCS, low FODMAP, gentle
- Corn Chex (plain) — same profile, different grain
- Cheerios (plain, not Honey Nut) — oat-based soluble fiber, low sugar
- Kix (plain) — corn-based, low sugar, low fiber

**Tier 2**
- Instant oatmeal, plain (Quaker, Bob's Red Mill) — prepped in <2 min, soluble fiber, add own sweetness
- Cream of rice — near-zero fiber, very gentle, mild sweet-with-butter-and-sugar tradition
- Puffed rice cereal with lactose-free milk — light, gentle

**Tier 3**
- Overnight oats with banana and maple syrup — different prep but same breakfast slot

---

### Instant oatmeal packets (sugary, flavored)
Common triggers: high sugar, sometimes artificial flavors, occasionally inulin (prebiotic fiber that's a FODMAP).

**Tier 1**
- Plain instant oats + maple syrup — same 90-second prep, controlled sweetness
- Plain quick oats cooked with banana mashed in — natural sweetness

**Tier 2**
- Cream of wheat (plain, add own toppings)

---

### Pastries and donuts (Pop-Tarts, toaster strudels, donuts, Danish)
Common triggers: trans fats, HFCS, high sugar, often wheat plus dairy, artificial colors.

**Tier 1**
- Toaster waffles (Kodiak Cakes plain, Van's gluten-free) topped with maple syrup — same toaster workflow, same sweet breakfast slot
- Cinnamon raisin English muffin with butter — warm sweet breakfast, lower sugar
- Baked cinnamon-sugar rice cakes (store-bought or homemade)

**Tier 2**
- Toast with honey and a thin layer of butter
- Blueberry muffin (bakery or homemade, smaller portion)

**Tier 3**
- Plain yogurt (lactose-free) with honey and a few berries — different food but sweet/creamy/quick

---

### Pancakes and waffles (sugar-heavy mixes, fast-food versions)
Common triggers: mix contains HFCS, excess sugar, sometimes partially hydrogenated oils; syrup is usually corn syrup.

**Tier 1**
- Kodiak Cakes pancake mix — oat/wheat protein, minimal sugar, real maple syrup on top
- Gluten-free pancake mix (Bob's Red Mill 1-to-1, King Arthur GF) — same form, no gluten
- Rice flour pancakes — very gentle, 3 ingredients
- Banana-egg-oat pancakes (3-ingredient classic)

**Tier 2**
- Real maple syrup instead of pancake syrup (Aunt Jemima/Mrs. Butterworth are HFCS) — swap just the syrup

---

### Breakfast sandwiches (McMuffin-style, frozen Jimmy Dean, fast food)
Common triggers: processed meat (sausage, bacon), melted processed cheese (often with emulsifiers), fried egg in seed oil, wheat bun.

**Tier 1**
- Homemade egg sandwich: English muffin + fried egg + turkey slice + aged cheddar — same form, dramatically cleaner
- Egg-on-toast with avocado (remission only — avocado is FODMAP-moderate) and a slice of turkey

**Tier 2**
- Scrambled eggs with a side of plain toast and a breakfast turkey sausage (Applegate Naturals nitrate-free)

---

### Granola bars (high-fiber, sugar-heavy, nutty, seedy)
Common triggers: inulin/chicory root fiber (FODMAP), sugar alcohols (quest, protein bars), tree nuts/seeds (insoluble fiber), dried fruit with sorbitol.

**Tier 1**
- Rice Krispies Treats (homemade or prepackaged plain) — low fiber, simple carbs, mild sugar
- Plain rice cake with a thin almond butter spread — similar "grab-and-go"
- Kind Breakfast bars (plain oat varieties, not the nut-heavy ones)

**Tier 2**
- Homemade oat bars (oats, maple syrup, banana) — controlled ingredients

---

### Yogurt cups (flavored, sugary, with fruit)
Common triggers: lactose, HFCS or added sugar, fruit-on-bottom with sorbitol, occasionally carrageenan.

**Tier 1**
- Lactose-free yogurt (Green Valley, Lactaid brand) — same form, pre-digested lactose
- Coconut yogurt (So Delicious, Cocojune plain) — dairy-free, live cultures
- Plain Greek yogurt (lower lactose than regular yogurt naturally) + own honey or maple

**Tier 2**
- Kefir (lactose-free variety) — drinkable yogurt-like

---

## Breads and grains

### White sandwich bread (Wonder Bread, Sunbeam, store brands)
Common triggers: HFCS, dough conditioners (often emulsifiers — monoglycerides, DATEM), sometimes preservatives.

**Tier 1**
- Sourdough bread — long fermentation breaks down FODMAPs, usually better tolerated; Monash University has tested this
- Dave's Killer Bread plain white (not the seed/nut-heavy varieties) — cleaner ingredients
- Canyon Bakehouse gluten-free white — GF, clean, familiar form

**Tier 2**
- Rice bread (Schär, Canyon Bakehouse) — very gentle, gluten-free

---

### Whole wheat / multigrain / seedy breads
Common triggers: Insoluble fiber and seeds can be difficult during flares or post-resection. (Note: during remission without obstruction risk, most Crohn's patients tolerate whole-grain bread fine.)

**Tier 1** (for flare or post-resection-sensitive users)
- Sourdough white
- Plain white sandwich bread, minimal ingredients
- Potato bread (Pepperidge Farm) — very soft, low insoluble fiber

---

### Bagels (especially seeded/whole grain, cream-cheese-heavy)
Common triggers: Dense wheat + seeds + high-lactose cream cheese combo.

**Tier 1**
- Plain white bagel + lactose-free cream cheese (Lactaid Cream Cheese, Green Valley Lactose Free)
- Mini plain bagel (smaller portion to manage portion)
- Thomas' English muffin (plain) — similar chewy-carb breakfast with less density

**Tier 2**
- Gluten-free plain bagel (Canyon Bakehouse) + lactose-free cream cheese

---

### Tortillas (flour)
Common triggers: Partially hydrogenated oils in shelf-stable ones, sometimes emulsifiers.

**Tier 1**
- Mission Plain Flour tortillas (original) — relatively clean ingredients, still wheat
- Siete almond-flour tortillas (cassava-based alternative; watch almond flour if nut-sensitive)
- Corn tortillas (plain) — gentler gluten-free option, though corn can bother some

---

## Pasta and noodles

### Regular wheat pasta
Common triggers: Gluten for sensitive users; some people tolerate well-cooked white pasta fine.

**Tier 1**
- Rice pasta (Jovial, Tinkyada) — closest to wheat pasta in texture and behavior
- Well-cooked (al dente is harder to digest; aim "soft-cooked") white pasta, small portion — often tolerated
- Corn-based pasta (Barilla corn/rice blend) — familiar texture

**Tier 2**
- Chickpea pasta (Banza) — higher FODMAP; may not work during flare, but high protein and well-tolerated by many in remission
- Lentil pasta — same caveat as chickpea

---

### Mac and cheese (boxed — Kraft, Velveeta)
Common triggers: Processed cheese with emulsifiers (polyphosphates, sodium citrate) and often artificial colors; wheat pasta.

**Tier 1**
- Annie's Homegrown white cheddar mac — real cheese, cleaner ingredient list
- Homemade mac with rice pasta + lactose-free cheddar (Lactaid slices work, Green Valley block cheese, aged cheddar is naturally low-lactose)
- Banza mac and cheese (GF, chickpea pasta — watch FODMAP tolerance)

**Tier 2**
- Pasta alfredo with lactose-free cream — same creamy-pasta craving

---

### Instant ramen / cup noodles
Common triggers: MSG, TBHQ, palm/hydrogenated oils, extreme sodium, often contains emulsifiers. Spice packets with capsaicin during flares.

**Tier 1**
- Rice vermicelli or rice noodle soup kits (Annie Chun's, Lotus Foods) — similar instant-prep, cleaner
- Pho kits (broth + rice noodles separate) — soothing, similar instant assembly

**Tier 2**
- Homemade rice noodles with bone broth and soft-cooked egg — 10 min, more nutritious

---

## Pizza

### Frozen pizza (DiGiorno, Totino's, Red Baron, Ellio's)
Common triggers: Processed cheese with emulsifiers, high-fat pepperoni/sausage, refined wheat crust, sometimes HFCS in sauce.

**Tier 1**
- Amy's Gluten-Free Dairy-Free pizza — same form, no major triggers
- Caulipower cauliflower-crust pizza — same eating occasion, no wheat, often dairy-free varieties
- Homemade pizza on sourdough flatbread base + lactose-free mozzarella + simple tomato sauce

**Tier 2**
- Flatbread "pizza" (Stonefire naan + sauce + cheese) — pizza-adjacent, different bread
- English muffin mini pizzas — classic home version

---

### Takeout/chain pizza (Domino's, Papa John's, pizza joint slices)
Common triggers: Very high fat, processed meats, potential FODMAP stack from onion/garlic, gluten.

**Tier 1**
- Cheese-only pizza from a pizzeria (remove pepperoni/sausage) — same experience, less fat
- Thin-crust pizza — less dough, faster digestion
- A single slice (not 3-4) — portion control often solves it alone

**Tier 2**
- Gluten-free crust from Domino's / Mellow Mushroom / local pizzeria

---

### Pizza rolls / Hot Pockets / pizza bagels
Common triggers: Same as frozen pizza plus often trans fats, TBHQ.

**Tier 1**
- Homemade pita pizzas (whole grain pita, sauce, cheese, quick bake) — same handheld form

---

## Meats and proteins

### Pulled pork (fatty, BBQ sauce often has HFCS and onion/garlic)
Common triggers: Very high fat (bile acid malabsorption = diarrhea), BBQ sauce FODMAP load, often cooked with beer or onions.

**Tier 1**
- Pulled chicken (slow-cooked chicken thighs or breast shredded) + homemade BBQ sauce — same sandwich form, dramatically lower fat
- BBQ jackfruit — vegan "pulled" texture; moderate FODMAP, small portions
- Shredded beef brisket lean cut (flat vs point) + clean BBQ — still beef, lower fat than pork shoulder

**Tier 2**
- Pulled turkey thigh — lean, juicy, same sandwich format

---

### Pot roast / beef brisket / fatty roasts
Common triggers: High fat, long-cooked with onion/garlic and wine, root veggies often include high-FODMAP ones.

**Tier 1**
- Crockpot chicken (skinless thighs or breast) with same veggies minus onion/garlic/celery — same "set-it-and-forget-it" meal
- Slow-roasted lean sirloin tip roast with carrots, potatoes, herbs (no onion/garlic)

---

### Bacon
Common triggers: Nitrates/nitrites (colorectal cancer concern), very high fat, sometimes sugar-cured, sodium.

**Tier 1**
- Turkey bacon (nitrate-free — Applegate, Wellshire Farms) — same crispy form, lower fat, cleaner
- Canadian bacon / Canadian ham — leaner cuts, still that cured flavor
- Center-cut pork bacon (nitrate-free Applegate Naturals) — real bacon, less fat, no added sugar or nitrites

**Tier 2**
- Prosciutto (small portion) — dry-cured, different but similar "salty pork strip" vibe with breakfast

---

### Sausage (breakfast links, bratwurst, Italian sausage)
Common triggers: High fat, often MSG, nitrates, spices, sometimes casings contain additives.

**Tier 1**
- Turkey breakfast sausage (Applegate, Jones Dairy Farm) — same form, lower fat
- Chicken apple sausage (Aidells — watch for onion/garlic) — similar flavor profile
- Homemade turkey sausage patties (ground turkey + sage + salt + maple) — clean and simple

---

### Deli meat / lunch meat (Oscar Mayer, Hillshire Farm — most)
Common triggers: Nitrates/nitrites, carrageenan, modified food starch, sodium phosphate, high sodium.

**Tier 1**
- Nitrate-free deli meats (Applegate Naturals, Boar's Head Simplicity, Hormel Natural Choice) — same slice-and-stack form
- Deli-counter fresh-roasted turkey breast (ask "no nitrates") — cleanest option

**Tier 2**
- Rotisserie chicken, cold, pulled for sandwiches — different form but same lunch protein slot

---

### Hot dogs / frankfurters
Common triggers: Nitrates, emulsifiers, corn syrup, very processed.

**Tier 1**
- Applegate Naturals uncured grass-fed hot dogs — same form, no nitrites, cleaner meat
- Ball Park / Nathan's nitrate-free line (check label) — same form, fewer additives
- Chicken or turkey hot dogs (nitrate-free varieties)

**Tier 2**
- Applegate chicken sausage (a sausage rather than a dog, but similar handheld meat)

---

### Salami, pepperoni, other dry cured meats
Common triggers: Nitrates/nitrites (most), very high fat, spices.

**Tier 1**
- Applegate uncured salami / pepperoni — same slices, no nitrites
- Prosciutto (nitrate-free from Italy is traditional curing)
- Sliced turkey (cold) with Italian herbs — different meat, similar sandwich role

---

## Fried foods

### Fried chicken (KFC, Popeyes, Bojangles, Chick-fil-A)
Common triggers: High fat, seed-oil frying (often fried in repeatedly reheated oil), spicy coating, wheat batter.

**Tier 1**
- Grilled chicken sandwich (same restaurants often have one) — same chain, same context
- Air-fried chicken at home (breaded in rice flour or panko; air-fried) — near-identical crispy texture, 80% less oil
- Baked "fried" chicken (coated, sprayed, oven-baked at 425°F)
- Rotisserie chicken, pulled and crisped in a skillet with a little oil

**Tier 2**
- Grilled chicken tenders (many restaurants have this)

---

### Chicken nuggets / tenders / strips (fast food and frozen)
Common triggers: Breading, seed-oil frying, often modified starches, sometimes flavor enhancers.

**Tier 1**
- Perdue Simply Smart baked — baked not fried, cleaner ingredients
- Applegate chicken nuggets (frozen, bake in oven) — cleaner
- Homemade: chicken strips dredged in rice flour or panko, air-fried or baked

---

### French fries / tater tots / onion rings
Common triggers: Seed-oil frying (often reheated), acrylamide from high-heat frying, salt, sometimes dextrose coating.

**Tier 1**
- Oven-baked French fries (Alexia, Ore-Ida Simply) — same form, half the oil
- Air-fried frozen fries at home
- Sweet potato fries (oven-baked) — similar form, vitamin A benefits (remission; may be tough during flare due to fiber)
- Baked potato wedges seasoned with olive oil and paprika

**Tier 2**
- Mashed potatoes (peeled, well-cooked) — potato craving, different form

---

### Fish sticks / fish fry / tempura
Common triggers: Seed-oil frying, batter ingredients including leaveners that some find bloating, sometimes sweetener in batter.

**Tier 1**
- Baked cod with a breadcrumb crust (homemade or frozen varieties) — same form, no deep-fry
- Air-fried fish fillets
- Grilled fish (a step further from fried but similar "fish for dinner" slot)

---

## Dairy

### Whole/2%/skim/chocolate milk
Common triggers: Lactose intolerance is extremely common in IBD (often exacerbated by active ileal inflammation).

**Tier 1**
- Lactaid brand (any fat %) — real dairy with lactase added; identical taste and nutrition
- Fairlife milk (ultra-filtered, lactose-free, higher protein) — excellent for those who like creamier milk
- A2 milk (Horizon, a2 Milk Company) — some lactose-intolerant-ish folks tolerate A2 protein better

**Tier 2**
- Oat milk (unsweetened, fortified — Oatly, Planet Oat, Califia) — low FODMAP in small servings, creamy, works in coffee
- Rice milk (unsweetened) — most hypoallergenic, thin texture
- Soy milk (Silk, Edensoy unsweetened) — high protein, dairy-free (note: higher FODMAP whole-soybean vs soy-protein milk — read labels)

**Avoid**
- Almond milk is fine as milk but watch calcium fortification and gums like carrageenan (older formulations; most are out now)

---

### Cream cheese (Philadelphia, etc.)
Common triggers: Lactose, sometimes gums.

**Tier 1**
- Green Valley Lactose Free cream cheese — same spreadability, same taste
- Lactaid cream cheese
- Kite Hill almond-based cream cheese — dairy-free, spreadable, similar savory bagel function

---

### Sour cream
Common triggers: Lactose.

**Tier 1**
- Green Valley Lactose Free sour cream — same function
- Tofutti Sour Supreme (dairy-free) — vegan option, similar consistency
- Plain lactose-free Greek yogurt — for dollops on tacos/potatoes, very close

---

### Hard cheese (cheddar, parmesan, gouda, swiss)
Note: Aged hard cheese has minimal lactose naturally (the aging fermentation breaks it down). Most Crohn's patients tolerate aged cheese fine. Usually not the primary trigger.

**Still look out for** pre-shredded cheese (anti-caking agents like cellulose and natamycin — some people notice issues) — shred your own.

---

### Soft cheese (mozzarella, brie, ricotta, cottage cheese)
Common triggers: Higher lactose than aged cheeses.

**Tier 1**
- Lactose-free mozzarella (Lactaid) — for pizzas, salads
- Lactose-free cottage cheese (Good Culture Classic — verify lactose-free line; some Good Culture varieties are naturally low-lactose)
- Feta (cow or sheep) — traditionally lower lactose

---

### Ice cream / Ben & Jerry's / Häagen-Dazs
Common triggers: Lactose, emulsifiers (polysorbate 80, carrageenan, guar gum in some), high fat, sometimes add-ins.

**Tier 1**
- Lactaid Ice Cream — real dairy, lactose pre-digested; familiar taste
- So Delicious coconut-milk ice cream — dairy-free, watch for inulin in some varieties
- Oatly / Van Leeuwen dairy-free — cleaner ingredients
- Nadamoo! coconut ice cream — simple ingredients
- Breyers Lactose-Free (select flavors) — widely available

**Tier 2**
- Sorbet (fruit-based, no dairy) — note high sugar, citrus-based ones often well-tolerated
- Homemade banana "nice cream" (frozen banana blended) — single ingredient, naturally creamy

**Avoid**
- Sugar-alcohol-sweetened "keto" ice creams (Halo Top, Rebel) — erythritol and other sugar alcohols cause osmotic diarrhea
- Gelato, traditional Italian — usually higher lactose than American ice cream despite less air

## Snacks

### Potato chips (Lay's, Ruffles, kettle chips, Pringles)
Common triggers: Seed oils, often MSG, sometimes emulsifiers in flavored varieties, salt.

**Tier 1**
- Baked potato chips (Lay's Oven Baked, Popchips) — same form, lower fat
- Kettle-cooked salted (just salt) potato chips — cleaner flavor list
- Homemade oven-baked potato slices with olive oil and salt

**Tier 2**
- Plain pretzels (Snyder's mini pretzels, Rold Gold) — similar salty crunch, lower fat

---

### Tortilla chips / Doritos / Cheetos
Common triggers: Cheese flavoring with emulsifiers + seed oils, MSG, sometimes maltodextrin.

**Tier 1**
- Plain tortilla chips (Tostitos Original, Juanita's) — same crunch, no flavor-dust
- Beanitos (bean-based, simpler) — higher fiber so try in remission only
- Corn tortilla cut and baked at home

**Tier 2**
- Plantain chips (Trader Joe's, Inka Crops) — similar crunchy sweet-salty snack

---

### Crackers (Ritz, Goldfish, Cheez-Its, Wheat Thins)
Common triggers: Trans fats / partially hydrogenated oils, artificial cheese flavoring, sometimes HFCS.

**Tier 1**
- Saltines (plain) — minimalist ingredients, very gentle
- Carr's Table Water Crackers — simple
- Late July organic saltines — cleaner
- Mary's Gone Crackers (GF, seed-based — check fiber tolerance)

**Tier 2**
- Rice crackers (plain) — GF, very gentle

---

### Pretzels
Already fairly gentle. Most store-bought are fine. Avoid honey-mustard-flavored or cheese-filled.

---

### Popcorn
Common triggers: Hulls can be mechanically irritating; post-resection patients often advised to avoid entirely due to obstruction risk in strictures; seed-oil microwave varieties add emulsifiers and artificial butter flavor.

**Tier 1 (if tolerated — verify with doctor for post-resection)**
- Air-popped popcorn with a little butter and salt — cleanest, but still has hulls
- Hull-less popcorn varieties (they exist but are rare) — Ladyfinger popcorn is naturally smaller-hulled

**Tier 2 (if avoiding popcorn entirely)**
- Puffed rice cakes (caramel or lightly salted) — similar light airy crunch, NO hulls
- Rice puff cereal eaten dry as a snack

---

## Sweet snacks and candy

### Gummy candy / Skittles / Starburst / Sour Patch Kids
Common triggers: HFCS or sugar bomb causing osmotic diarrhea, artificial colors, sometimes gelatin sensitivity, extreme acid (sour varieties).

**Tier 1**
- Organic gummy candies without artificial colors (Black Forest Organic, YumEarth, Annie's Organic Fruit Snacks) — same chewy-sweet form, cleaner ingredients
- Fruit leather / fruit strips (That's It, Stretch Island) — chewy, single-ingredient fruit, natural sugars in moderation
- Natural fruit gummies (Welch's Juicefuls, Organic Valley Whole Fruit Straws) — cleaner sweet

**Tier 2**
- Dried mango (unsweetened, no sulfites added; Trader Joe's has this) — chewy, sweet, tropical
- Dates (Medjool) — naturally candy-sweet texture, but a genuine reframe

**Avoid**
- "Sugar-free" gummies (Haribo sugar-free is infamous for diarrhea — maltitol/sorbitol)

---

### Hard candy (Jolly Ranchers, lollipops, Life Savers)
Usually sugar + flavor + color. Minor GI risk beyond sugar; sour versions can trigger reflux.

**Tier 1**
- Organic hard candies (YumEarth, Torie & Howard) — same form, no artificial colors/flavors
- Ginger chews (Prince of Peace) — surprisingly candy-like and actually anti-nausea

---

### Chocolate bars (Snickers, Twix, Kit Kat, M&M's, Reese's)
Common triggers: High fat (especially with peanuts/caramel), dairy (lactose), HFCS, emulsifiers (soy lecithin is usually fine, PGPR in some bars), sugar alcohols in "lite" versions.

**Tier 1**
- Dark chocolate bar 70%+ (small portion) — less sugar, less dairy, flavonoids
- Enjoy Life dairy-free chocolate bars or chips — cleaner, lactose-free
- Hu Chocolate — paleo, clean, "rich dark chocolate" category
- Justin's dark chocolate peanut butter cups — cleaner than Reese's

**Tier 2**
- Chocolate-dipped fruit (strawberries, bananas) — same chocolate craving, less total volume

---

### Cookies (Oreos, Chips Ahoy, bakery cookies)
Common triggers: Trans fats (less common now but check), HFCS, wheat + dairy, artificial flavors.

**Tier 1**
- Tate's Bake Shop chocolate chip cookies — cleaner ingredients, real butter
- Trader Joe's Joe-Joe's — Oreo-alternative, cleaner
- Gluten-free chocolate chip cookies (Tate's GF, Kinnikinnick, homemade with GF flour)
- Homemade oatmeal cookies — soluble fiber, controlled sugar

**Tier 2**
- Shortbread (plain butter cookies) — simple ingredient list
- Rice crackers drizzled with melted dark chocolate — lighter, similar sweet crunch

---

### Ice cream bars / frozen novelties (Klondike, Magnum, drumsticks)
Common triggers: Same as ice cream plus cone can have emulsifiers; chocolate coating often has PGPR.

**Tier 1**
- So Delicious / Nada Moo! dairy-free ice cream bars — similar form
- Outshine fruit bars — fruit-based, refreshing, no dairy, lower fat
- Homemade banana dipped in melted dark chocolate and frozen on a stick

---

## Beverages

### Soda (Coke, Pepsi, Sprite, Mountain Dew, Dr Pepper)
Common triggers: HFCS (osmotic load), phosphoric acid (mild), caffeine (colon motility), carbonation (bloating in some).

**Tier 1**
- Sparkling water with a squeeze of citrus (LaCroix, Spindrift, Bubly) — same fizzy refreshing drink
- Zevia (stevia-sweetened soda) — soda form, watch stevia tolerance
- Olipop / Poppi "prebiotic" sodas — cleaner soda alternative; caveat: they contain inulin/chicory root which is a FODMAP and can backfire for some CD patients
- Diet soda (Coke Zero, Pepsi Zero) — same soda experience; acesulfame/aspartame are generally OK but sucralose has microbiome concerns in some studies

**Tier 2**
- Flavored sparkling water with a splash of 100% fruit juice — color, flavor, minimal sugar
- Kombucha (low-sugar brands like GT's Synergy Trilogy) — fizzy, probiotic benefit

**Tier 3**
- Iced herbal tea — different category, similar refreshing role

---

### Energy drinks (Red Bull, Monster, Rockstar, Bang, Celsius)
Common triggers: Very high caffeine (GI motility), artificial sweeteners, sometimes taurine/guarana, carbonation, sugar alcohols in sugar-free versions.

**Tier 1**
- Brewed green tea or black tea on ice — gentler caffeine, L-theanine balances jitters
- Yerba mate (loose or Guayakí brand cans) — energy drink format, cleaner
- Plain cold brew coffee — natural caffeine, no additives

**Tier 2**
- Coconut water with a squeeze of lemon + a pinch of salt — natural electrolyte refresh

---

### Coffee (fancy lattes with syrups, Starbucks Frappuccinos)
Common triggers: Lactose in milk, HFCS in syrups (bottled Frappuccinos), caffeine motility effects for some.

**Tier 1**
- Plain drip coffee + lactose-free milk + own syrup control
- Decaf version of same drink — 97% less motility stimulation; useful during active flares
- Latte made with oat milk or lactose-free milk

---

### Fruit juice (OJ, apple, grape, cranberry cocktails)
Common triggers: Concentrated sugar causing osmotic diarrhea, fructose load, sometimes HFCS in cocktails.

**Tier 1**
- Diluted 100% juice (half water, half juice) — same flavor, half the sugar load
- Coconut water (plain, unsweetened) — natural electrolytes, lower sugar than juice
- Low-sugar fruit spritzer: sparkling water + splash of juice

**Tier 2**
- Herbal fruit tea iced, unsweetened

---

### Beer / wine / liquor
Alcohol has direct mucosal irritant effects, increases intestinal permeability, and interacts with many Crohn's medications (absolute contraindication with methotrexate; strong caution with azathioprine). See `knowledge/12-lifestyle-triggers.md` for full details.

**Beer → Tier 1 alternatives**
- Non-alcoholic beer (Athletic Brewing, Brooklyn Special Effects, Heineken 0.0) — same form, no alcohol
- Hop water (HOP WTR, Lagunitas Hoppy Refresher) — IPA-like bitter herbal flavor, zero alcohol
- Low-sugar kombucha — fizzy and slightly acidic, adult-drink feel

**Wine → Tier 1**
- Dealcoholized wine (Ariel, Leitz, Noughty) — polyphenols preserved, no alcohol
- Sparkling grape juice (Welch's) — celebratory feel

**Liquor / cocktails → Tier 1**
- Zero-proof spirits (Seedlip, Ritual) — cocktail format, no alcohol
- Virgin mojito, virgin G&T, other mocktails — same drink occasion

## Condiments and sauces

### Ketchup
Most mainstream brands use HFCS. Risk is usually moderate (small serving sizes).

**Tier 1**
- Primal Kitchen ketchup (sweetened with dates) — same flavor, no HFCS, cleaner
- Heinz Simply Organic (cane sugar, not HFCS)
- Tessemae's ketchup

---

### BBQ sauce (Sweet Baby Ray's, Kraft)
Common triggers: HFCS, onion/garlic (often high FODMAP), molasses, sometimes liquid smoke.

**Tier 1**
- Primal Kitchen BBQ — cleaner sweeteners, no HFCS, some varieties low-FODMAP-compatible
- Tessemae's Matty's BBQ — cleaner

### Hot sauce (Sriracha, Tabasco, Buffalo)
Capsaicin is the main trigger for active flares; many Crohn's patients tolerate hot sauce fine in remission.

**Tier 1 (if avoiding capsaicin during flare)**
- Ginger-based sauces — adds kick, anti-inflammatory
- Mustard-based sauces (Carolina-style BBQ, Dijon) — tang without heat
- Horseradish sauce — sinus heat without gut capsaicin

**Tier 2**
- Small amount of hot sauce during remission — often tolerated; test tolerance

## Things with no good Tier 1 swap — flag as "limit or avoid"

Some foods simply don't have a Crohn's-safe "same food" alternative. Better to label clearly than invent a bad swap.

- **Spicy wings (Buffalo, ghost pepper)** — during flares, no. In remission, a small portion of mild-sauced wings often works.
- **Raw kale / raw Brussels sprouts / other tough raw cruciferous** — don't try to swap, recommend well-cooked versions or different vegetable entirely.
- **Heavily mushroom-based dishes** — post-resection mushroom cellulose walls are hard; no clean swap.
- **Large portions of beans/lentils** — for active-flare users with FODMAP sensitivity, no. In remission, small portions often OK.
- **Unripe bananas or apple skins** — for stricture patients, obstruction risk is the issue; swap is "peel and cook" or different fruit.
- **Popcorn for post-resection patients with known stricture** — honest answer: don't eat it.

## Gaps and honest notes

- **Specific brand availability varies by region.** Mentioning "Applegate Naturals" presumes US groceries — a UK or Canadian user will see different brands. Future work: surface "search for nitrate-free turkey bacon at your local grocery" rather than specific brands when available.
- **Not all brand formulations are stable over time.** A brand that was clean in 2025 may reformulate. The engine should be updated annually.
- **Individual tolerance varies enormously.** A universally "Tier 1" swap may still bother an individual user. The app's personal tolerance tracking should eventually override this static library.
- **Some Tier 1 suggestions have FODMAP caveats** (oat milk in large amounts, Banza chickpea pasta, prebiotic sodas with inulin). These are noted inline but may warrant stronger flags in UI.
- **This file does not cover pharmaceutical or supplement replacement** — that belongs in `05-medications-detailed.md` and `13-complementary-medicine.md`.
