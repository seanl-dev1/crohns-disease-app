# Peripheral Arthritis — Type 2 (Polyarticular)

Reference file for Claude. Technical language. Evidence graded where available.

## EIM: Peripheral Arthritis Type 2

The Orchard TR et al. classification (*Gastroenterology* 1998;114(3):478-485) subdivides IBD-associated peripheral arthritis.

**Type 2 — Polyarticular**
- **Joints**: >5 joints; small joints; symmetric; upper extremities (MCPs especially)
- **Course**: Chronic, months to years
- **Gut correlation**: **Independent** of bowel disease activity
- **Prevalence**: ~2-4% of IBD
- **HLA**: Associated with HLA-B44
- **Treatment**: Often requires dedicated therapy — anti-TNF (adalimumab, infliximab have strongest evidence), upadacitinib, sometimes sulfasalazine.

**Type 3 (Smale classification)** — combined axial + peripheral features.

### Key pearl
IBD peripheral arthritis is typically **non-erosive and non-deforming** (contrast to RA). Absence of bony erosion on imaging does not rule it out.

### NSAIDs and IBD
- **ECCO 2024**: Avoid non-selective NSAIDs when possible in IBD due to risk of flare induction/exacerbation.
- **Mechanism**: COX-1 inhibition → disruption of mucosal prostaglandins.
- **Alternatives**: Acetaminophen (paracetamol) first line for analgesia. **COX-2 selective** inhibitors (celecoxib, etoricoxib) appear safer in short courses per small RCT data, but still avoid prolonged use.
- Topical NSAIDs are generally safe.

### Treatment Principle
Peripheral arthritis Type 2 often runs independently of luminal activity and typically requires its own directed therapy. It often needs a biologic with dual indication (anti-TNF) or a separate therapy in co-management.

### Specialist Referral
Rheumatology referral is appropriate for peripheral arthritis Type 2, suspected axSpA, or refractory joint symptoms.
