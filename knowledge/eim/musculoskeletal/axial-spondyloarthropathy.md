# Axial Spondyloarthritis (axSpA) / Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS)

Reference file for Claude. Technical language. Evidence graded where available.

## EIM: Axial Spondyloarthropathy

- **Prevalence**: Sacroiliitis on imaging in **20-50%** of IBD patients (often asymptomatic); clinically symptomatic AS in **3-10%** of CD.
- **HLA-B27**: Present in ~50-70% of IBD-associated AS (lower than primary AS at 74-89%); HLA-B27 status in IBD does not reliably predict axial disease.
- **IBD strongly predicts positive MRI-sacroiliitis**: In one cohort, IBD raised pre-test probability of MRI sacroiliitis from 41% to 81%.
- **Course**: Runs **independently** of bowel disease; can progress even when gut is quiescent.
- **Symptoms**: Inflammatory back pain — >3 months, onset <45 years, morning stiffness >30 min, improves with activity, worsens with rest, nocturnal awakening.
- **Diagnosis**: MRI sacroiliac joints (bone marrow edema, erosions) is gold standard for early disease; X-ray sacroiliitis is later finding.
- **Treatment**:
  - Physiotherapy is foundational.
  - **Anti-TNF** (infliximab, adalimumab, certolizumab, golimumab) — strong evidence for both gut and axial disease.
  - **Etanercept is NOT effective** for luminal IBD despite being effective for AS — avoid in IBD-AS.
  - **IL-17 inhibitors (secukinumab, ixekizumab)** — effective for AS but may precipitate or worsen IBD; **generally avoided** in IBD-AS.
  - **IL-23 inhibitors (risankizumab, ustekinumab)**: emerging evidence in IBD; ustekinumab has modest effect on axial symptoms [LOW EVIDENCE for axial].
  - **JAK inhibitors (upadacitinib, tofacitinib)**: dual efficacy, approved for both AS and UC (upadacitinib also for CD).

### Treatment Principle
Axial SpA / AS typically needs its own or additional therapy — often runs independently of luminal activity and requires anti-TNF or JAK with dual indication.

### When to Worry / Referral Trigger
- Inflammatory back pain >3 months, age <45 → MRI SI joints, HLA-B27, rheumatology referral.
