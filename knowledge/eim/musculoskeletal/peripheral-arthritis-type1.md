## EIM: Peripheral Arthritis Type 1

The Orchard TR et al. classification (*Gastroenterology* 1998;114(3):478-485) subdivides IBD-associated peripheral arthritis.

**Type 1 — Pauciarticular**
- **Joints**: ≤5 joints; large, asymmetric; lower extremities (knees, ankles, hips)
- **Course**: Acute, self-limited (<10 weeks typically)
- **Gut correlation**: **Parallels** bowel activity; often precedes/heralds flare
- **Prevalence**: ~4-17% of IBD
- **HLA**: Associated with HLA-B27, HLA-B35, HLA-DR103
- **Treatment**: Treat the gut flare. Acute NSAID use is controversial (see below). Intra-articular steroids, short-course systemic steroids, anti-TNF if severe.

### Key pearl
IBD peripheral arthritis is typically **non-erosive and non-deforming** (contrast to RA). Absence of bony erosion on imaging does not rule it out.

### NSAIDs and IBD (relevant to arthritis management)
- **ECCO 2024**: Avoid non-selective NSAIDs when possible in IBD due to risk of flare induction/exacerbation.
- **Mechanism**: COX-1 inhibition → disruption of mucosal prostaglandins.
- **Alternatives**: Acetaminophen (paracetamol) first line for analgesia. **COX-2 selective** inhibitors (celecoxib, etoricoxib) appear safer in short courses per small RCT data, but still avoid prolonged use.
- Topical NSAIDs are generally safe.

### Treatment Principle
Peripheral arthritis Type 1 typically responds to gut-targeted therapy — activity tracks with bowel inflammation; treat the gut.
