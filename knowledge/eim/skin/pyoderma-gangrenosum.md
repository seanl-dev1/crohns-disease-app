# Pyoderma Gangrenosum (PG)

Reference file for Claude. Technical language. Evidence graded where available.

## EIM: Pyoderma Gangrenosum

- **Prevalence**: **1-2% in IBD** (up to 5% in some cohorts); more common in UC than CD.
- **Appearance**: Starts as pustule/papule, rapidly progresses to painful ulcer with **violaceous undermined border**; most often on shins, peristomal sites.
- **Pathergy**: Lesions worsen with trauma/debridement — **avoid surgical debridement**.
- **Course**: Often **independent** of gut activity; can occur during remission.
- **Diagnosis**: Clinical + biopsy of edge (neutrophilic dermatosis; no specific finding); exclude infection, vasculitis, malignancy.
- **Treatment**:
  - **Systemic corticosteroids** (prednisone 0.5-1 mg/kg/day) — first-line for most patients.
  - **Cyclosporine** — alternative first-line.
  - **Infliximab** — supported by a **randomized controlled trial** (Brooklyn TN et al. *Gut* 2006;55(4):505-509): 46% improvement at 2 weeks with infliximab 5 mg/kg vs. 6% placebo; 69% improved at 6 weeks (21% complete resolution). **ECCO and most algorithms list infliximab as first-line for IBD-associated PG**.
  - Adalimumab, ustekinumab, and more recently JAK inhibitors have case-series support.
  - Topical tacrolimus or intralesional steroids for small lesions.

### Treatment Principle
PG often needs its own/additional therapy — typically independent of luminal activity. Infliximab has strongest RCT support.

### When to Worry
- Painful rapidly expanding skin ulcer with violaceous border → Pyoderma gangrenosum (don't debride — pathergy). Dermatology; systemic steroid + infliximab.
