# Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA)

Reference file for Claude. Technical language. Evidence graded where available.

## EIM: Iron Deficiency Anemia

Anemia is the most common EIM (or "complication").
- **Prevalence**: **~24% of IBD patients** have anemia (pooled; higher in CD, in active disease, and in hospitalized patients — up to 68%).

**Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA)** — most common
- **ECCO ferritin cutoffs**:
  - Mild disease / remission (no inflammation): **ferritin <30 µg/L** = iron deficiency.
  - Active inflammation (CRP elevated): **ferritin <100 µg/L + TSAT <20%** = iron deficiency likely (inflammation elevates ferritin as acute-phase reactant, masking deficiency).
  - Ferritin >100 + TSAT <20% = anemia of chronic disease (functional iron deficiency).
- **Oral iron**: Ferrous sulfate 65 mg elemental daily or every other day (every-other-day dosing improves fractional absorption). Can worsen GI symptoms.
- **IV iron**: Indicated for intolerance, severe anemia (Hb <10 g/dL), active flare, hepcidin blockade, Hb inadequate response to oral iron by 4 weeks. Options: ferric carboxymaltose (Ganz-Katz protocol), iron isomaltoside, ferumoxytol, iron sucrose.
- **Maintenance / reinitiation**: Restart IV iron if ferritin falls <100 µg/L or Hb <12 g/dL (ECCO).

**Folate Deficiency** (often co-exists)
- **Prevalence**: ~22% in CD.
- **Causes**: Methotrexate antagonism (mandatory folate supplementation 1-5 mg/day on MTX), sulfasalazine (competitive inhibitor), malabsorption (jejunal disease), poor intake.
- **Testing**: RBC folate more reliable than serum folate for chronic status.
- **Treatment**: Folic acid 1-5 mg/day.

### When to Worry
- Unexplained anemia → IDA (common), ACD, B12 deficiency. Ferritin + TSAT + CRP, B12, folate; IV iron if active disease.

### Monitoring
- **Ferritin + TSAT** (use ECCO cutoffs) at annual review.
