# Yoga for IBD — What the RCTs Show

**Scope:** Landmark Cramer 2017 UC yoga RCT, Sharma 2015, narrative reviews, style-specific guidance (restorative/yin/hatha/Iyengar preferred vs. vinyasa/hot yoga cautions), and pose-by-pose considerations during active disease.

**Evidence grading shorthand:**
- **[A]** — Multiple RCTs / systematic review of RCTs, consistent findings
- **[B]** — At least one RCT or several well-designed non-randomized studies
- **[C]** — Small uncontrolled studies, case series, or extrapolation from adjacent fields
- **[D]** — Expert opinion / mechanistic reasoning only

## 8. Yoga for IBD — What the RCTs Show

### 8.1 Cramer et al. 2017 — landmark yoga RCT

**Cramer H, Schäfer M, Schöls M, et al. Randomised clinical trial: yoga vs written self-care advice for ulcerative colitis.** *Aliment Pharmacol Ther*. 2017;45(11):1379–1389. **PMID 28378342.**

- n=77 adults with UC in clinical remission + impaired QoL
- Randomized to 12 weekly 90-min supervised hatha yoga sessions vs. written self-care booklet
- **Primary outcome (QoL, IBDQ):** significantly higher in yoga group at 12 and 24 weeks
- Secondary: disease activity (Clinical Activity Index) lower in yoga group at 24 weeks
- Mediating variable (Langhorst et al., 2019, secondary analysis PMID 31927346): effect mediated by reduced perceived stress
- Adverse events comparable between groups
- **Evidence grade: [A]** for UC in remission; extrapolation to Crohn's **[C]**

### 8.2 Sharma et al. 2015 — yoga in IBD

**Sharma P, Poojary G, Dwivedi SN, Deepak KK. Effect of yoga-based intervention in patients with inflammatory bowel disease.** *Int J Yoga Therap*. 2015;25(1):101–112. **PMID 26667293.**

- Smaller mixed IBD sample
- 8-week yoga intervention → improved anxiety, pain, IBS-like symptoms; less clear effect on disease activity
- Methodologically weaker than Cramer

### 8.3 Narrative reviews

- Balaji et al. *Crohns Colitis 360*, 2022 (PMC 9802393) — yoga effective for QoL and mental health in IBD; insufficient evidence for disease remission maintenance
- Other supportive small trials report reductions in depression, anxiety, perceived stress; benefits on fatigue reported but variable

### 8.4 Style and pose-specific guidance

**Favored styles in IBD:**
- **Restorative / Yin** — parasympathetic dominance, low mechanical stress, safe during mild flares
- **Gentle Hatha** — the style used in Cramer trial; the most evidence-supported
- **Iyengar** — props emphasize alignment and sustainable holds

**More caution:**
- **Vinyasa / Power / Ashtanga** — appropriate in remission with good baseline fitness; higher intensity
- **Hot yoga / Bikram** — dehydration risk; generally inadvisable in IBD with ongoing fluid losses

**Pose considerations:**
- **Emphasize:**
  - Child's pose (balasana) — gentle abdominal release
  - Supine twists (supta matsyendrasana) — gentle GI motility support
  - Cat–cow — spinal mobility with controlled abdominal movement
  - Legs up the wall (viparita karani) — venous return, parasympathetic
  - Bridge (setu bandhasana) — glute/hamstring activation, mild back extension
  - Diaphragmatic pranayama (see breathing-vagal-tone.md)
- **Avoid or modify during active disease:**
  - Deep forward folds with abdominal compression (full paschimottanasana, janu sirsasana with full flexion)
  - Bow pose (dhanurasana), locust (salabhasana) — deep anterior abdominal compression
  - Full boat (navasana) at maximum effort — rectus abdominis maximal contraction
  - Inversions (headstand, shoulder stand) — if uncontrolled hypertension, retinopathy, severe reflux, or perianal hemorrhoids
  - Deep twists with abdominal compression in the presence of strictures
