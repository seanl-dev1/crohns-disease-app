## 12. Hydration and Electrolytes for Exercise with Crohn's

### 12.1 Who is at elevated risk

- **Post–ileal resection** (>100 cm lost, approximately) — risk of BAM and fluid/electrolyte malabsorption
- **Short bowel syndrome** (<200 cm remaining small bowel) — major losses; often on ORS
- **Active disease with diarrhea** — ongoing daily losses
- **Stoma patients** — high-output ileostomy (>1.5 L/day) especially vulnerable

### 12.2 The "drink more water" trap

Drinking plain hypotonic fluid (water, tea, diet soda) in SBS / high-output ostomy settings **worsens losses** because the gut secretes sodium into the lumen to match osmotic gradient, pulling water with it.

Reference: Parrish C, UVA Ginutrition practitioner guide; St Mark's solution PDF (Imperial NHS); Nightingale, *Practical Gastroenterology*, SBS hydration 2019 (PMC 5718176).

### 12.3 Optimal ORS characteristics

- **Sodium:** 90–120 mmol/L (higher than WHO standard ORS used for childhood diarrhea, which targets 75 mmol/L)
- **Glucose:** equimolar with sodium (~1:1) — activates SGLT1 co-transport for optimal absorption
- **Osmolality:** isotonic or slightly hypotonic (~240–290 mOsm/L)
- **St Mark's solution recipe (classic):**
  - 1 L water
  - 20 g glucose (or 40 g sucrose)
  - 3.5 g NaCl (sodium chloride)
  - 2.5 g NaHCO₃ (sodium bicarbonate)
  - Total Na+ ~90 mmol/L, Cl– ~60 mmol/L, HCO₃– ~30 mmol/L, glucose ~110 mmol/L

### 12.4 Commercial options

- **WHO-ORS sachets** (lower sodium than ideal for SBS but available)
- **DripDrop, Liquid I.V.** — consumer ORS; sodium ~500–1000 mg/L range
- **Pedialyte** — closer to WHO-ORS; sodium ~1035 mg/L (~45 mmol/L) — borderline for SBS
- **LMNT** — 1000 mg sodium per stick; used by many athletes; adequate sodium for heavy-sweat or moderate malabsorption contexts
- **Plain coconut water** — insufficient sodium (~250 mg/L); high potassium; not appropriate as sole ORS for SBS

### 12.5 Electrolyte targets for IBD exercisers

| Electrolyte | Context | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Sodium** | Active exercise, post-resection | 1000–2000 mg/h during prolonged exercise; total daily need may be 4–6 g |
| **Potassium** | Long sessions, stoma output | 300–600 mg/h during prolonged exercise; dietary emphasis |
| **Magnesium** | Common deficiency in IBD | 300–400 mg/day; glycinate/citrate well-tolerated; oxide causes diarrhea |
| **Calcium** | Bone health | 1000–1200 mg/day; not exercise-acute |
| **Zinc** | Wound healing, immunity | 8–11 mg/day; higher if high-output stoma |

### 12.6 Sean-specific hydration note

Sean, as a long-post-resection patient (~20 years), may have residual subclinical bile-acid malabsorption (see running-practical-considerations.md and contraindications.md). For him:
- Pre-, intra-, and post-exercise sodium emphasis is reasonable even without classic SBS
- Monitor morning body weight trends during training blocks — acute loss >1 kg often = fluid debt
- Urine color: pale straw as baseline target
- Daily sodium intake probably ~3–5 g minimum during training phases
