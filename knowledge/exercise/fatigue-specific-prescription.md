# Fatigue-Specific Exercise Prescription (IBD)

**Scope:** Why exercise paradoxically reduces fatigue, GET vs pacing distinction, post-exertional malaise flag, symptom-titrated exercise, and a specific week-by-week starting protocol for the deconditioned fatigued IBD patient.

**Evidence grading shorthand:**
- **[A]** — Multiple RCTs / systematic review of RCTs, consistent findings
- **[B]** — At least one RCT or several well-designed non-randomized studies
- **[C]** — Small uncontrolled studies, case series, or extrapolation from adjacent fields
- **[D]** — Expert opinion / mechanistic reasoning only

## 15. Fatigue-Specific Exercise Prescription

### 15.1 Why exercise reduces fatigue (paradoxically)

- **Central mechanism:** improved cerebral BDNF, dopaminergic/serotonergic function
- **Mitochondrial biogenesis:** endurance training increases mitochondrial density, partly reversing central fatigue
- **Reduced systemic inflammation:** see anti-inflammatory-mechanisms.md §2.1
- **Improved sleep architecture:** deeper slow-wave sleep with regular exercise
- **Reduced deconditioning:** the "energy envelope" expands with training

### 15.2 Graded exercise therapy (GET) — cautions

**Important distinction:** GET in traditional CFS/ME literature has been controversial (NICE 2021 guidance de-emphasized GET for ME/CFS). For IBD fatigue without post-exertional malaise (PEM) pattern, graded progressive exercise is generally appropriate. When PEM pattern exists (symptom worsening 24–72 h after exertion, disproportionate to intensity), **pacing** is preferred over GET.

### 15.3 Pacing strategy

- **Activity budget concept:** Estimate daily "energy envelope" in units (1 unit = 5–15 min of moderate activity).
- **Use 70–80%** of the envelope consistently, preserving margin for setbacks.
- **HR-based pacing:** Stay below ~60% HRmax or anaerobic threshold during bad days.
- **Interval-of-rest approach:** Microbreaks (30–90 sec sit or standing rest) every few minutes during light activities.
- **Avoid the "push–crash" cycle** — big activity day → multi-day bed rest → deconditioning → worse fatigue.

### 15.4 Symptom-titrated exercise (evidence-supported variant)

- Martin et al., *Sci Rep*, 2024 — symptom-titrated exercise RCT in post-COVID showed fatigue/QoL improvement
- **Principle:** Progress volume only when previous week tolerated without symptom worsening. If worsened, drop back 1–2 steps and re-advance.
- Weekly progression example: +5 min walking time OR +1 rep per set, not both. Monitor 2-week rolling average rather than single bad/good day.

### 15.5 Starting prescription for deconditioned fatigued IBD patient

**Week 1–2:** Walking 5–10 min 3–5x/day (light conversational pace). Diaphragmatic breathing 2x 5 min. No resistance.
**Week 3–4:** Combine into 15–20 min walks 3x/day. Add resistance — bodyweight squats 2 × 8, wall push-ups 2 × 8, bird dog 2 × 5/side, 2x/week.
**Week 5–8:** 30 min walking 4–5 days/week. Resistance 2x/week with 6 exercises, 2 × 10.
**Week 9–12:** Add light intervals (3 × 1 min brisk / 1 min easy) 1x/week. Progress resistance volume.
**Beyond:** Full ACSM-style prescription (see exercise-in-remission.md §3.1) if tolerated.
