# When NOT to Exercise — Crohn's-Specific Contraindications

**Scope:** Absolute and relative contraindications to exercise in Crohn's — bowel obstruction, severe anemia, abscess, acute post-op, toxic megacolon, dehydration, active GI bleeding, DVT, perianal abscess, and undifferentiated pelvic/abdominal pain, plus relative cautions (high CRP, known stricture, fluoroquinolones, steroids, PEM-pattern fatigue).

**Evidence grading shorthand:**
- **[A]** — Multiple RCTs / systematic review of RCTs, consistent findings
- **[B]** — At least one RCT or several well-designed non-randomized studies
- **[C]** — Small uncontrolled studies, case series, or extrapolation from adjacent fields
- **[D]** — Expert opinion / mechanistic reasoning only

---

## 17. When to NOT Exercise

Absolute or near-absolute contraindications:

| Condition | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Active or suspected bowel obstruction** | Exertion + Valsalva may worsen distension; abdominal pain + vomiting + no passage of stool/gas = ED visit, not the gym |
| **Severe anemia (Hb <8–9 g/dL)** | Inadequate oxygen delivery; risk of cardiac ischemia at exertion; treat first |
| **Acute intra-abdominal abscess** | Mechanical compression and thermogenesis may propagate infection |
| **Major surgery <4–6 weeks** | Wound dehiscence, hernia, adhesive complications |
| **Uncontrolled cardiovascular disease** | Unstable angina, uncontrolled arrhythmia, decompensated heart failure, severe aortic stenosis |
| **Acute toxic megacolon** | Life-threatening; ICU-level care, not exercise |
| **Severe dehydration / electrolyte disturbance** | Correct first; cardiac arrhythmia risk |
| **Active GI bleeding** | Stabilize, transfuse, investigate — not exercise |
| **Uncontrolled DVT** (on pre-therapeutic anticoagulation) | Embolization risk |
| **Active perianal abscess requiring drainage** | Pressure-based activity (cycling, rowing, squatting deep) may worsen; light walking ok |
| **Severe pelvic or abdominal pain of unclear etiology** | Investigate before exercising through it |

Relative cautions (dial intensity down, discuss with GI):
- CRP markedly elevated, active inflammation per labs, regardless of symptoms
- Known high-grade stricture (even asymptomatic) + vigorous training → stricture may become obstructive with transit alteration
- Fluoroquinolone course (see exercise-and-medications.md §14.5)
- Prednisone >20 mg/day for >4 weeks (load management, tendon caution)
- Severe fatigue with PEM pattern (pacing, not GET — see fatigue-specific-prescription.md)
