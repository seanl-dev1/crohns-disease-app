# Post-Surgical Exercise Timeline for Crohn's Surgery

**Scope:** Day-0-through-lifelong exercise progression after Crohn's surgery (resection, laparotomy, laparoscopy). ERAS-based ambulation, healing windows, graded resumption, hernia risk, and Sean-specific residual considerations at ~20 years post-resection.

**Evidence grading shorthand:**
- **[A]** — Multiple RCTs / systematic review of RCTs, consistent findings
- **[B]** — At least one RCT or several well-designed non-randomized studies
- **[C]** — Small uncontrolled studies, case series, or extrapolation from adjacent fields
- **[D]** — Expert opinion / mechanistic reasoning only

## 5. Post-Surgical Exercise Timeline

### 5.1 Immediate post-operative (day 0–7)

- **Walking encouraged from day 1.** Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocols emphasize early ambulation to reduce VTE risk, ileus duration, and atelectasis.
- **Initial targets:** 3–5 short walks/day (5–10 min), progressing to 15–20 min as tolerated.
- **No lifting >4–5 kg (10 lb) in first week** (laparoscopic) / stricter for open laparotomy.

### 5.2 Weeks 1–6 — the critical healing window

- **Collagen remodeling at incision site** reaches ~70% of ultimate tensile strength by 6 weeks.
- **Restrictions (open/major resection):**
  - No lifting >4.5–6.8 kg (10–15 lb)
  - No direct abdominal loading (crunches, sit-ups, planks, Pilates hundreds)
  - No Valsalva / maximal strain activities
  - Avoid high-impact running and jumping
- **Laparoscopic** (less tissue disruption): some surveys support as little as 2 weeks reduced activity (Frontiers Surg, PMC 8511488), though 4–6 weeks is the commonly recommended conservative window. **[B/C]**
- **Permitted by week 2–4:** Recumbent or upright stationary cycling (low resistance), swimming once incision fully healed (usually ~3 weeks, confirm no open wound), elliptical at low intensity.

### 5.3 Weeks 6–12 — graded resumption

- Reintroduce resistance training at ~50% of pre-op loads; emphasize form and full ROM.
- Core training begins with diaphragmatic breathing → dead bugs → bird dogs → progressive anti-rotation and anti-extension work.
- Avoid maximal-effort breath-holding; exhale through the effort phase.
- Compound lifts (squats, deadlifts, bench press) typically safe by 8–12 weeks; keep weights conservative initially.

### 5.4 Beyond 12 weeks — lifelong hernia-risk considerations

- **Incisional hernia rates:** ~10–20% at 2 years post-open abdominal surgery; higher with wound infection, steroids, obesity, malnutrition.
- **Lifelong caution** for very heavy lifting (1RM deadlifts, max back squats) in unconditioned patients; not a strict prohibition but demands proper progression, bracing technique, and awareness of bulging or pain at old incision.
- **Port-site hernias** after laparoscopy are possible — typically where trocars >10 mm were placed.
- Reference: Kjøbli/Benjamin et al., international survey on post-abdominal-surgery activity (PMC 8511488).

### 5.5 Sean-specific note

Sean is ~20 years post-resection — well beyond all acute surgical restrictions. Relevant residual considerations:
- Incision-site tissue weaker than uninvolved abdominal wall for life → respect proper bracing with heavy lifts
- Potential for adhesive obstruction — occasional cramping with heavy meals + vigorous exercise warrants medical attention, not just "push through"
- Bile acid malabsorption possible post-ileal resection (see hydration-electrolytes.md)
