# Exercise During Crohn's Remission — What's Safe

**Scope:** General aerobic + resistance + flexibility prescription during remission (ACSM-style, IBD-adapted), HIIT evidence in mild/remission Crohn's, modality-by-modality tradeoffs for team sports, running, cycling, swimming, and more.

**Evidence grading shorthand:**
- **[A]** — Multiple RCTs / systematic review of RCTs, consistent findings
- **[B]** — At least one RCT or several well-designed non-randomized studies
- **[C]** — Small uncontrolled studies, case series, or extrapolation from adjacent fields
- **[D]** — Expert opinion / mechanistic reasoning only

---

## 3. Exercise During Remission — What's Safe

### 3.1 General prescription (extrapolated from ACSM + IBD-specific data)

**Aerobic:**
- **Frequency:** 3–5 days/week
- **Intensity:** Moderate (40–60% VO₂R, ~RPE 12–14/20, "talk test" preserved) — most robust evidence base
- **Time:** 30–60 min continuous, or accumulated in 10-min bouts
- **Type:** Walking, cycling, swimming, elliptical, rowing — low- to moderate-impact preferred
- **Total volume:** 150 min/week moderate or 75 min/week vigorous (aligns with WHO/ACSM general guidance)

**Resistance:**
- **Frequency:** 2–3 non-consecutive days/week
- **Intensity:** Start 50–60% 1RM; progress to 60–80%
- **Volume:** 1–3 sets × 8–12 reps, 6–10 major muscle group exercises
- **Progression:** 2–10% weekly load increase when top of rep range reached with good form

**Flexibility / neuromotor:** 2–3 days/week (yoga, tai chi, mobility).

### 3.2 HIIT in IBD

- **Evidence [B]:** Tolerated and safe in mild/remission Crohn's (Tew 2019 PMID 30696423; single-session pediatric data, Cronin et al.). No exacerbation of fecal calprotectin 24 h or 1 week post-event in endurance athletes with CD (small prospective series).
- **Caveats:**
  - Data do not extend to moderate–severe active disease.
  - Consider symptom-titration approach in first 6–12 weeks of training.
  - Pediatric CD data show monocyte inflammatory response differs from healthy controls but does not translate to clinical flare.

**Reasonable protocol example:** 10 × 1 min at 85–95% HRmax with 1 min active recovery; 2x/week; alongside 1–2 sessions continuous moderate work. Progress from 4–6 intervals initially.

### 3.3 Team sports / running / cycling / swimming — tradeoffs

| Modality | Pros | Cons / Cautions |
|---|---|---|
| **Walking** | Universal safe baseline, low-impact, cumulative | Limited stimulus for VO₂max gains; need progression |
| **Running** | High BMD stimulus, time-efficient, cardioprotective | Runner's diarrhea (see running-practical-considerations.md); high mechanical impact on abdomen; bathroom planning |
| **Cycling** | Low-impact, controllable intensity | Saddle pressure uncomfortable with active perianal disease or hemorrhoids |
| **Swimming** | Zero-impact, whole-body, cooling effect | Pool access; ostomy/pouch considerations (see exercise-with-ostomy.md); locker-room bathroom proximity |
| **Rowing / elliptical** | Low-impact, high aerobic stimulus | Forward-folded rowing posture can aggravate acute abdominal pain |
| **Team sports** | Social engagement, motivation | Scheduling unpredictable; contact sports have abdominal trauma risk, especially with stoma |
| **Resistance training** | BMD, sarcopenia prevention, metabolic | Valsalva and hernia considerations (see resistance-training.md) |
| **Yoga / Pilates** | Flexibility, stress reduction, parasympathetic | Deep abdominal compression (e.g., bow, full locust) contraindicated in active disease |
