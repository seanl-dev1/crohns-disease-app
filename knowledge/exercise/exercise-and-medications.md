# Exercise and Medications in Crohn's

**Scope:** Exercise interactions with biologics, corticosteroids (including steroid myopathy reversal), thiopurines/methotrexate, JAK inhibitors (VTE signal + ORAL Surveillance), antibiotics (fluoroquinolone tendinopathy), and aminosalicylates.

**Evidence grading shorthand:**
- **[A]** — Multiple RCTs / systematic review of RCTs, consistent findings
- **[B]** — At least one RCT or several well-designed non-randomized studies
- **[C]** — Small uncontrolled studies, case series, or extrapolation from adjacent fields
- **[D]** — Expert opinion / mechanistic reasoning only

## 14. Exercise and Medications

### 14.1 Biologics (anti-TNF, anti-integrin, anti-IL-12/23)

- No significant exercise contraindications
- No dose-timing restrictions around workouts
- Injection-site considerations only: avoid heavy bag/resistance work on injection arm in first 24 h if soreness
- Biologics improve disease activity → typically enable higher exercise tolerance

### 14.2 Corticosteroids (prednisone, budesonide)

- **Corticosteroid-induced myopathy** (StatPearls NBK557731): proximal pelvic girdle weakness, type 2b fiber atrophy; dose-dependent (>10 mg prednisone/day for >4 weeks elevates risk); typically painless; normal CK
- **Resistance training prevents and partially reverses this** (Horber et al., *J Clin Invest*, 1985; PMID 3998075 — prednisone-induced myopathy reversed by physical training) **[A]** for the phenomenon; **[B]** for intervention
- **Practical implications during steroid courses:**
  - Increase resistance training frequency (3x/week)
  - Emphasize lower body compound movements (hip/knee extension loading)
  - Additional protein (up to 1.6–2.0 g/kg) to offset catabolism
  - Vitamin D 2000–4000 IU/day (often co-prescribed)
  - Calcium 1000–1200 mg/day
  - Tendon/ligament tensile strength may be reduced during steroid courses — progress loads conservatively; avoid max efforts
- **Avascular necrosis (AVN)** risk, particularly femoral head — hip pain during/after steroid course warrants imaging before progressive loading

### 14.3 Azathioprine / 6-mercaptopurine / methotrexate

- Monitor for fatigue, cytopenias (CBC q3mo)
- Methotrexate: avoid alcohol around training days; consider day-after-dose light sessions if fatigue pattern
- No direct exercise contraindications at therapeutic doses

### 14.4 JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, upadacitinib, filgotinib)

- **FDA boxed warning (2021)** for JAK inhibitors: increased MACE, malignancy, VTE, mortality vs TNFi — based on **ORAL Surveillance** (Ytterberg et al., *NEJM*, 2022; PMID 34965625) in RA patients ≥50 with ≥1 CV risk factor
- **VTE signal is dose-dependent:** HR ~3.52 at tofacitinib 10 mg BID (ORAL Surveillance); milder at 5 mg BID. IBD indication uses 10 mg BID induction, 5–10 mg BID maintenance.
- **Exercise implications:**
  - No direct exercise restriction in current guidelines
  - **Long-haul travel + JAK + sedentary** = elevated VTE risk context; patients on JAK inhibitors should hydrate, move every 60–90 min during travel
  - **Maintain regular aerobic exercise** as part of baseline VTE risk mitigation
  - **Avoid prolonged immobilization** (post-procedure recovery periods — consult prescriber)
  - Discuss with GI if planning high-altitude or extreme-endurance events
- Risk factors to flag: prior VTE, BMI ≥35, age ≥65, chronic lung disease, known thrombophilia

### 14.5 Antibiotics (metronidazole, ciprofloxacin for perianal disease)

- **Fluoroquinolones** (cipro, levofloxacin): tendinopathy and tendon rupture risk — particularly Achilles. **Avoid high-strain tendon loading, plyometrics, heavy jumping, maximal sprints during and for 2–4 weeks after course.**
- Metronidazole: GI disturbance, neuropathy with long courses; no direct exercise effect

### 14.6 Aminosalicylates (mesalamine)

- Minimal exercise interaction
- Rare nephrotoxicity warrants hydration vigilance
