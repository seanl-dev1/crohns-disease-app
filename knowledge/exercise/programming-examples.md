# Programming Examples for Crohn's — Sean's Situation

**Scope:** Weekly maintenance template, deconditioning recovery phases, flare-appropriate movement menu, and long-term focus areas for a ~20-year post-resection Crohn's patient. Plus gaps in the evidence base and a summary evidence-strength table.

**Evidence grading shorthand:**
- **[A]** — Multiple RCTs / systematic review of RCTs, consistent findings
- **[B]** — At least one RCT or several well-designed non-randomized studies
- **[C]** — Small uncontrolled studies, case series, or extrapolation from adjacent fields
- **[D]** — Expert opinion / mechanistic reasoning only
- **[Weak]** — Marked inline where the evidence base is particularly thin

## 16. Practical Programming for Sean's Situation

### 16.1 Baseline assumptions

- Long-post-resection (~20 yr) — well past acute surgical restrictions
- Likely subclinical bile-acid malabsorption consideration (see hydration-electrolytes.md, running-practical-considerations.md)
- Unknown current fitness baseline — programming here errs on the side of graded entry
- Age likely enters the "maintain/prevent sarcopenia" zone regardless of Crohn's

### 16.2 Maintenance program (remission, moderate fitness)

**Weekly template:**
- **Monday:** Full-body resistance, 45 min. 5 compound + 1 core. 3 sets of 8–10, RPE 7.
- **Tuesday:** 30–40 min zone 2 cardio (walking, cycling, swim). Breathing 10 min.
- **Wednesday:** Full-body resistance or mobility/yoga (gentle hatha).
- **Thursday:** Interval session — 5 × 3 min at RPE 7–8 with 2 min recovery. Or hill walk.
- **Friday:** Rest or gentle walk / yoga.
- **Saturday:** Longer cardio session 45–75 min low–moderate; include some impact (jogging, jump rope, hiking with varied terrain) for BMD.
- **Sunday:** Mobility, tai chi, or complete rest.

### 16.3 Deconditioning recovery (post-flare or long sedentary stretch)

**Weeks 1–4:** fatigue-specific-prescription.md §15.5 protocol.
**Weeks 5–8:** Consolidate walking base (30–45 min most days). Resistance 2x/week, 2 sets. Add 1 yoga/mobility.
**Weeks 9–16:** Layer in zone 2 cycling/swimming. Resistance 3x/week, progress to 3 sets. Add impact (jump rope doubles, 5 min). First running re-introduction 1 min run / 2 min walk intervals.

### 16.4 Flare-appropriate movement

During a mild flare:
- Walking 10–20 min 2–3x/day, conversational pace
- Restorative yoga 15–20 min (supported child's pose, legs up wall, supine twist, viparita karani)
- Breathing practice 10–15 min (4-7-8 or box breathing)
- Very light bodyweight movement: 1 set × 5 squats, 1 set × 5 wall push-ups — preserve motor pattern

Halt and consult GI if:
- New severe abdominal pain
- Fever
- Bloody diarrhea new or escalating
- Vomiting
- Persistent worsening fatigue despite rest

### 16.5 Long-term focus areas specific to Sean

1. **Bone density maintenance** — resistance + impact 2–3x/week
2. **Sarcopenia prevention** — protein 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day, resistance 2–3x/week
3. **Cardiovascular health** — zone 2 majority, some zone 4 intervals
4. **Autonomic regulation** — daily breathing practice, weekly yoga or tai chi
5. **Injury prevention with aging joint/tendon tissue** — consistent mobility, moderate plyometric load
6. **Sustainability over maximum intensity** — the program that gets done for decades beats the program that gets done hard for 8 weeks

## 18. Summary Table — Evidence Strength Across Key Claims

| Claim | Grade | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Moderate exercise improves fatigue in IBD | A/B | Consistent across trials |
| Exercise reduces disease activity (moderate effect) | B | Jones 2022 meta-analysis, SMD ~–0.44; low certainty |
| Physical activity lowers IBD incidence (prevention) | B | Tiong 2024 meta-analysis, Crohn's RR 0.78–0.87 |
| Exercise improves BMD in Crohn's | B | Robinson 1998 + Jones 2020 RCTs |
| Yoga improves QoL in UC | A | Cramer 2017 RCT |
| Yoga improves QoL in Crohn's specifically | C | Extrapolation |
| HIIT safe in mild/remission Crohn's | B | Tew 2019 pilot RCT |
| Resistance training prevents steroid myopathy | B | Non-IBD-specific but mechanistically consistent |
| Whey + leucine + resistance benefits sarcopenia in IBD | B/C | Strong in general sarcopenia; IBD extrapolation |
| Tai chi/qigong improves IBD fatigue | Weak / D | No direct IBD RCTs; mechanism and adjacent-field support |
| Pelvic floor biofeedback improves IBD fecal incontinence | C | Non-randomized IBD-specific data + RCTs in non-IBD populations |
| Vagal-activating breathing reduces IBD inflammation | D | Mechanism plausible; direct clinical RCT evidence sparse |
| Support belts prevent parastomal hernia | C | Observational data strong; RCT pending |
| JAK inhibitors + exercise: no restriction needed | D | Extrapolated; no exercise-specific trial |

## 19. Gaps I Couldn't Verify

The following topics have **limited high-quality IBD-specific evidence** or where my sources were indirect/adjacent. These need explicit caveat when used in answers:

1. **Tai chi / qigong RCTs in IBD populations specifically.** All evidence cited is from ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, or generic chronic illness. No dedicated IBD RCT identified in the search.
2. **Effect of HIIT on disease activity in moderately active Crohn's.** Tew 2019 excluded active disease. Extrapolation to active disease is speculative.
3. **Optimal protein intake specifically for active resistance-training Crohn's patients** (vs. non-exercising IBD patients). The 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day figure is derived from ESPEN IBD + ISSN athlete recommendations; no head-to-head trial in IBD exercisers.
4. **Long-term safety of heavy lifting post-resection at 20+ year interval.** Surgical literature focuses on first 12–24 months. The lifelong incisional hernia residual risk is real but incremental absolute risk from heavy lifting in remote post-op patients is not well quantified.
5. **JAK inhibitor–specific VTE risk modulation by exercise status.** ORAL Surveillance reported the risk signal but did not stratify by physical activity level. Assumption that exercise lowers risk is based on general VTE epidemiology, not trial data.
6. **Whether prophylactic loperamide before exercise is safe long-term in Crohn's.** Widely used empirically among IBD runners; I found no RCT of prophylactic loperamide in IBD exercisers.
7. **Effect of specific yoga styles on disease activity** — Cramer used gentle hatha. Whether vinyasa, Iyengar, or Ashtanga produce different disease-activity outcomes is untested.
8. **Pelvic floor rehabilitation in post-proctectomy / pouch patients specifically.** Most IBD PFMT evidence is in patients with intact anatomy. Pouch patients likely benefit but are under-represented in trials.
9. **Exercise-induced microbiome changes in IBD specifically.** Moderate exercise increases SCFA-producing taxa in general populations; IBD-specific human data are sparse and observational.
10. **Detailed VO₂ / METs targets for post-surgical return in Crohn's.** Extrapolated from general post-abdominal-surgery rehab; no Crohn's-specific staged cardiovascular return-to-activity guidelines identified.
11. **Specific sodium dosing for post-resection exercisers (non-SBS).** St Mark's solution designed for SBS; lighter post-resection without SBS may need less but empirical guidance is by trial-and-error clinically.
12. **Safety of inversions (headstand, shoulder stand) in IBD** with reflux, hiatal hernia, or hemorrhoids — mostly based on yoga-tradition caution; no IBD-specific studies.
