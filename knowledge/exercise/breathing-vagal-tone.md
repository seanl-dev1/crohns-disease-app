# Breathing Exercises and Vagal Tone in IBD

**Scope:** Physiology of vagal afferent stretch, respiratory sinus arrhythmia, resonance frequency breathing, diaphragmatic/4-7-8/box/slow-exhale protocols, and gut-specific relevance (cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway, motility, visceral hypersensitivity).

**Evidence grading shorthand:**
- **[A]** — Multiple RCTs / systematic review of RCTs, consistent findings
- **[B]** — At least one RCT or several well-designed non-randomized studies
- **[C]** — Small uncontrolled studies, case series, or extrapolation from adjacent fields
- **[D]** — Expert opinion / mechanistic reasoning only

## 11. Breathing Exercises and Vagal Tone

### 11.1 Physiological basis

- The **vagus nerve** (CN X) carries ~80% afferent (gut → brain) and ~20% efferent (brain → gut) fibers.
- Diaphragmatic excursion mechanically stretches vagal afferents; slow exhalation maximally activates parasympathetic outflow.
- **Respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA)** — HR rises with inspiration, falls with expiration; deeper breathing = larger RSA = higher HRV = better vagal tone.
- **Resonance frequency breathing** (~0.1 Hz = 6 breaths/min) maximally amplifies baroreflex gain and parasympathetic activity.

Reference: Gerritsen & Band, *Front Hum Neurosci*, 2018 (PMC 6189422) — respiratory vagal stimulation model; Magnon et al. *Sci Rep*, 2021 — deep slow breathing increases HF-HRV and reduces anxiety.

### 11.2 Protocols (all evidence [B/C] for general population; extrapolated to IBD [C])

| Protocol | Pattern | Use case |
|---|---|---|
| **Diaphragmatic breathing** | Belly expands on inhale, 4–6 sec in, 6–8 sec out, nasal breathing | Daily baseline; gut-directed |
| **4-7-8 breathing** | 4 in / 7 hold / 8 out | Sleep onset, acute anxiety, pre-bathroom urgency |
| **Box breathing** | 4-4-4-4 | Stress-down-regulation during workday |
| **Resonance breathing** | ~6 breaths/min, roughly 5 in / 5 out | HRV biofeedback training |
| **Slow exhale emphasis** | 1:2 in:out ratio | Acute parasympathetic boost |

### 11.3 Gut-specific relevance

- Vagal activation → cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway → reduced TNF-α from macrophages
- Gut motility normalization (reduces both delayed gastric emptying in some IBD patients and rapid colonic transit in others)
- Reduces visceral hypersensitivity — relevant for IBD–IBS overlap, ~30–50% of IBD patients
- Integrates with gut-directed hypnotherapy, progressive muscle relaxation, and mindfulness-based interventions evidenced in IBD (UCLA and others — §integrative gastroenterology)

### 11.4 Practical prescription

- **Baseline:** 5 min diaphragmatic breathing 2x/day (morning, pre-bed)
- **Acute urgency management:** 4-7-8 for 4 cycles; often defers urge long enough to reach bathroom or complete transit
- **Pre-exercise:** 5 slow diaphragmatic breaths — improves HRV and may reduce sympathetic GI symptoms
- **Pre-sleep:** extended exhale, 10 min
