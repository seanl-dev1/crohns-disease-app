# Tai Chi / Qigong for IBD — Emerging Evidence

**Scope:** State of the (sparse) IBD-specific evidence, borrowed evidence from ME/CFS/fibromyalgia/chronic pain, mechanistic plausibility (vagal tone, low METs, BMD, stress reduction), and practical prescription.

**Evidence grading shorthand:**
- **[A]** — Multiple RCTs / systematic review of RCTs, consistent findings
- **[B]** — At least one RCT or several well-designed non-randomized studies
- **[C]** — Small uncontrolled studies, case series, or extrapolation from adjacent fields
- **[D]** — Expert opinion / mechanistic reasoning only

## 9. Tai Chi / Qigong — Emerging Evidence

### 9.1 IBD-specific evidence — sparse

**No adequately-powered RCTs specifically on tai chi or qigong for IBD.** Current best estimates derive from:
- Adjacent chronic-illness conditions (ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, chronic pain, post-COVID)
- Mechanistic parasympathetic tone / HRV literature

### 9.2 Adjacent-condition evidence

- **Wang C et al. *NEJM*, 2010** — tai chi for fibromyalgia: significant fatigue, pain, depression improvements vs wellness control
- **Markwart et al. *Glob Adv Integr Med Health*, 2024 (PMC 11544658)** — systematic review on qigong/tai chi for ME/CFS: consistent fatigue reduction (Chalder Fatigue Scale, MFI-20)
- **Chronic-illness meta-analyses** (Jogh.org 2023; Science Direct reviews) — tai chi broadly improves HRQoL in chronic conditions

### 9.3 Mechanistic plausibility for IBD fatigue

- Autonomic rebalancing (vagal tone ↑, sympathetic ↓)
- Low metabolic cost (~2–4 METs) — accessible during partial recovery and mild flare
- Weight-bearing, modest BMD benefit
- Stress reduction through meditative movement

**Practical prescription [C/D]:**
- 20–30 min, 3–5 sessions/week
- Minimum 9 weeks to see effects per qigong/ME-CFS meta-analyses
- Accessible apps: Tai Chi Foundation resources; online Yang-style 24-form video instruction
