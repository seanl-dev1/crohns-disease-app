# Exercise During a Crohn's Flare — What's Safe

**Scope:** Intensity-layered movement prescription during mild/moderate/severe flares, red-flag signs to stop, and restorative low-impact options.

**Evidence grading shorthand:**
- **[A]** — Multiple RCTs / systematic review of RCTs, consistent findings
- **[B]** — At least one RCT or several well-designed non-randomized studies
- **[C]** — Small uncontrolled studies, case series, or extrapolation from adjacent fields
- **[D]** — Expert opinion / mechanistic reasoning only

---

## 4. Exercise During a Flare — What's Safe

### 4.1 General principles

- **Mild flare** (Crohn's Disease Activity Index, CDAI 150–220; or Harvey–Bradshaw Index 5–7): light–moderate exercise often tolerated; reduce intensity ~30–50%, shorten duration, favor low-impact.
- **Moderate flare** (CDAI 220–450; HBI 8–16): restorative movement only (gentle walking, chair yoga, breathing, light mobility). Avoid resistance training progression and HIIT.
- **Severe flare** (CDAI >450; HBI >16; systemic symptoms, severe pain, bloody diarrhea, fever): rest. Exercise is contraindicated; focus on medical stabilization.

The Hoffman & Raftery 2023 commentary ("Intensity-specific considerations for exercise for patients with IBD," *Gastroenterology Report*) supports this intensity-layered approach. **[C/D]** — based on expert consensus and limited direct trial data.

### 4.2 Signs to stop or step down

- Worsening abdominal pain during or after exercise (not typical DOMS)
- Increased stool frequency, urgency, or blood
- Unusual fatigue persisting >24 h after normal exercise (post-exertional malaise pattern)
- Fever or night sweats appearing with exertion
- Orthostatic dizziness (consider anemia or dehydration)
- New joint swelling/pain (possible extraintestinal manifestation flare)

### 4.3 Low-impact options during flare

- **Walking** — start at 5–15 min, RPE 9–11; even very short bouts preserve conditioning and improve mood.
- **Restorative yoga** — supported poses, minimal core/abdominal compression.
- **Tai chi / qigong** — low metabolic cost, balance and vagal tone benefit (see tai-chi-qigong.md).
- **Aquatic exercise** — buoyancy offloads joints; warm water (~32–34°C) reduces muscle tension. Contraindicated if perianal fistula with active drainage.
- **Breathing practices** (see breathing-vagal-tone.md) — essentially no contraindications short of pneumothorax.
- **Gentle resistance with very light loads / bands** — preserves motor pattern without significant inflammation.
